Anthropic’s IPO draws more demand than SpaceX’s ahead of Thursday’s debut

Dario Amodei, chief executive of Anthropic, at Fortune Brainstorm Tech in Park City, Utah, in July 2023.

In a recent development, orders for Anthropic’s IPO have reached close to seven times the shares on offer, people familiar with the matter told Bloomberg on Sunday. Demand is much hotter than it was for SpaceX at the same stage, one of them noted. The only comparable deal, that person said, was Snowflake’s 2020 listing, whose shares jumped 112% on their first day.

In a recent development, goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, which lead the deal, expect to close the order book on Tuesday, Bailey Lipschultz and Carmen Reinicke reported. That is the day Nvidia reports its first results without Jensen Huang. The Anthropic IPO is set to price on Wednesday. The shares should start trading on the Nasdaq under ANT on Thursday.

Anthropic and some of its backers are raising up to $90.4bn, counting a $26bn private placement. That would beat SpaceX’s $75bn base deal and make it the biggest listing ever. The manufacturer set its IPO terms on 10 November, at $70 to $78 a share and a $1.4tn valuation.

Orders were about twice the shares available by the end of the roadshow’s first day, 10 November, according to Reuters. By Thursday demand had hit six times the offer, Milana Vinn, Echo Wang and Krystal Hu reported. Bloomberg had reported a similar figure a day earlier, alongside the founders’ share sale.

Recent findings suggest that not every investor is welcome. Banks have turned away orders from institutions in mainland China and Hong Kong, Bloomberg reported on 13 November. US defence-trade rules known as ITAR are part of the reason, some of its sources said. Some are trying other routes, including a trust in the British Virgin Islands and swap arrangements, the people said.

Investors on the roadshow asked whether Anthropic can keep its lead as Google and others catch up, Reuters reported. They also asked whether Claude can win beyond coding. One investor said the manufacturer had not given detailed answers to some questions. Another said one group meeting had no dial-in line for questions, which breaks with normal practice.

Industry observers note that the valuation rests on forecasts. Banks put Anthropic’s 2028 revenue at about $270bn to $280bn, two people told Reuters. One investor who passed on the deal uses a 2028 figure about 30% lower. Goldman Sachs, which has forecast $685bn in revenue by 2030, does not expect positive annual free cash flow until then, according to Bloomberg.

Dario Amodei took some of those questions in public on 13 November, at a livestreamed JPMorgan event with Jamie Dimon. He stated buying computing power a year or two ahead was his hardest call, Business Insider reported.

“Compute is the deepest business dilemma that the tech giant faces,” Amodei said.

According to recent reports, he remarked Anthropic buys relatively little compute in advance. If demand runs high, its cash lets it buy more on the spot market. Its commitments top $900bn, with about $71bn due by September 2027, Bloomberg reported.

Recent findings suggest that howard Marks, co-founder of Oaktree Capital, doubts any AI leader can stay ahead for long. He made the case in a memo last week.

“If companies can forge ahead that fast in the world of AI, can they likewise be supplanted by others, or at least challenged?” he wrote.

In a recent development, anthropic’s public-benefit structure leaves it wide room to define what counts as benefit, governance experts told the Associated Press. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a majority of the board, but the founders keep 50.1% of the general vote. A protest group, Stop Funding Billionaires, has demonstrated outside Anthropic’s offices in Fresh York and San Francisco, Business Insider reported.

Industry observers note that nvidia has agreed to buy about $10bn of the private placement. Huang resigned as chief executive on 12 November, five days before the order book closes.
