Anthropic researcher claims Claude helped him prove the Riemann hypothesis

In a recent development, mmathematician at Anthropic says he has used the manufacturer’s Claude AI model to prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics. Levent Alpöge made the claim in a note on his website on Friday, Bloomberg reported.

“I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,” Alpöge wrote.

According to recent reports, the hypothesis, first proposed in 1859, concerns how prime numbers are spread out. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, each of which carries a $1m prize. Alpöge says his proof also covers a broader version, the generalised Riemann hypothesis.

According to recent reports, he did not publish a traditional paper. Instead, he released the proof in Lean, a programming language that checks every logical step. The code runs to about 17 million lines, Science reported.

Sources indicate that andrew Sutherland, a senior research scientist at MIT, told Bloomberg he had checked the code on his own computer. He mentioned he had seen at least three other checks by professional mathematicians. Later on Friday, Sutherland put the chance that anything is wrong at roughly zero, Scientific American reported.

In a recent development, however, other mathematicians urged caution. A proof that compiles in Lean still has to state the right problem, and humans have to confirm that it does.

Kevin Buzzard, a professor at Imperial College London, mentioned nobody yet understands what the proof demonstrates, Al Jazeera reported. The proof has not been formally accepted.

Alpöge said Claude did much of the work.

“This time Claude far outpaced me near the finish line,” he wrote.

Anthropic declined to comment to Bloomberg. The claim came a day after the company’s record stock market debut.

Recent findings suggest that if confirmed, it would be the second Millennium Prize Problem that AI may have helped solve. In September, OpenAI mentioned its models had produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem.
