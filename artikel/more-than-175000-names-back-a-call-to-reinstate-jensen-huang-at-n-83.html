More than 175,000 names back a call to reinstate Jensen Huang at Nvidia

  • It asks the board to reinstate Huang while an independent investigation runs.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang at NVIDIA GTC 2026 in San Jose.

It has been revealed that an open letter asking Nvidia’s board to bring back Jensen Huang had more than 175,000 names on Monday, four days after he resigned as chief executive. The Stand with Jensen site counted 8,766 people who say they work at Nvidia. It puts that at about 21% of the company’s 42,000 staff. Every affiliation on the site is self-reported, and TNW has not verified the signatures.

The letter makes three demands. It asks the board to reinstate Huang while an independent investigation runs. It wants the inquiry’s scope and timeline disclosed, with a hearing for Huang before any final decision. And it asks for a public explanation of the concerns behind his exit.

“Jensen deserves due process, not mob justice,” the letter indicates.

The site also urges institutional investors to raise the matter on Nvidia’s earnings call on Tuesday, 17 November, at 5pm ET.

In a recent development, the site lists Elon Musk first among its public signers, dated 13 November, the day after Huang stepped down. It also indicates 26 people who describe themselves as vice presidents or above at Nvidia. The letter notes it is not endorsed by Nvidia or by Huang.

It has been revealed that the count has not been clean. Business Insider reported on Saturday that the organisers, who post as @TheJensenLetter on X, were removing fake signatures. The tally then stood at more than 108,000.

Sources indicate that the letter is one of several moves by Huang’s allies. Investors Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker wrote to the board on Friday. On Sunday, the Financial Times reported that Huang had discussed a comeback with confidants. TNW covered both in its report on the return talks.

Industry observers note that the letter says no evidence has shown that the woman at the centre of the case was a spy. A separate report deals with what Huang told his own executives.

It has been revealed that reuters reported on 14 November that Huang misled several Nvidia leaders about his relationship with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang. They included general counsel Tim Teter and chief marketing officer Alison Wagonfeld. Reporters Max A. Cherney and Jeffrey Dastin cited two people familiar with the matter and internal messages from October that the agency reviewed.

In those messages, Huang described his dealings with Alice Huang as standard business with a customer, according to Reuters. She was a senior executive at Megaspeed, the Singapore manufacturer that bought Nvidia chips, and the two are not related. The company said on Sunday that she left in August 2025.

Reuters also reported that Huang discussed legal action against The New York Times over an October 2025 article about their public meetings. No suit was filed. A Times spokesperson told Reuters the paper received no legal demand.

Industry observers note that nvidia’s independent directors hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz on 10 November to run the board’s investigation. Nvidia told CNBC on Sunday that it was choosing a search firm for the chief executive role and had not formally engaged any candidates.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent spoke about Alice Huang on Fox News Channel’s Sunday Morning Futures. He noted whether she is a spy “doesn’t matter to me”. His concern, he said, was whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance with US export restrictions.

Wedbush analyst Dan Ives told CNBC he does not expect a return.

“We see no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO,” Ives said.

In a recent development, colette Kress, the interim chief executive, presents Nvidia’s quarterly results on Tuesday. It is her first public appearance in charge, according to The Information. TNW found no public response from the board to the letter.
