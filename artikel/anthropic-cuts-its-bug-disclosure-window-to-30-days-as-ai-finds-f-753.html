Anthropic cuts its bug disclosure window to 30 days as AI finds flaws

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (425 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that anthropic is giving software makers less time to fix the security flaws it reports.
  • Industry observers note that the new deadlines apply to flaws reported on or after 1 January 2027.
  • Recent findings suggest that anthropic is making two other changes.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that anthropic is giving software makers less time to fix the security flaws it reports. AI models now find bugs much faster, it says. Its Frontier Red Team said on Monday it will publish serious flaws 30 days after reporting them, down from 90 days. That covers high- and critical-severity flaws. All other flaws get 45 days.

Industry observers note that the new deadlines apply to flaws reported on or after 1 January 2027. Reports already in progress keep their old deadlines.

Recent findings suggest that anthropic is making two other changes. If a maintainer does not answer its first report, it will go to an outside coordinator after 15 days instead of 30. And once a patch is out, it will wait 30 days instead of 45 before sharing full technical details.

In a recent development, some serious flaws Anthropic reported over 90 days ago are past its current deadline and still not public. Anthropic indicates it is telling those maintainers they have 30 more days. After that, it will publish the reports on its dashboard under the latest policy from 1 January 2027.

In a recent development, “A 90-day window made sense when a vulnerability could be expected to stay private for months. We no longer believe it can,” Anthropic wrote.

The old rules assumed fixes moved at human speed and that two people rarely found the same bug, the tech giant mentioned. Now a bug one model finds is more and more likely to be found by another, it said.

It has been revealed that some flaws take longer than 30 or 45 days to fix, such as those in critical infrastructure software or in a technical standard. In those cases, Anthropic says it will agree a timeline with the vendor. Its seven-day window for flaws under active attack stays the same. The policy does not cover AI findings that no person has reviewed, such as those from its OSS Scanner service.

According to recent reports, anthropic notes shorter deadlines may put more pressure on maintainers. It says it will keep sending fix guidance, and a patch where it can, with each report.

Google’s Project Zero is shortening its disclosure window too, SecurityWeek reported on Monday. From 2 February 2027, it will publish flaws 60 days after reporting them. From 3 January 2028, the window drops to 30 days.
