Databricks in early talks to raise at a $225bn valuation, WSJ reports

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ali Ghodsi, co-founder and chief executive of Databricks.

It has been revealed that databricks is in early talks to raise several billion dollars at a valuation of about $225bn, The Wall Street Journal’s Meghan Bobrowsky and Kate Clark reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. That would be nearly 20% above the $190bn valuation the data firm reached in August.

The talks are ongoing and the terms could still change, according to the Journal. TNW has not independently verified the report.

In a recent development, the fresh money would come while Databricks stays private. Its chief executive, Ali Ghodsi, explained why in a recent interview on the All-In podcast.

Industry observers note that “We will go public. We are going to be public, but right now I think during this transition period of AI there’s just too much up and down, and I think it’s distracting to companies,” Ghodsi told All-In.

Databricks closed a $5bn round at $190bn in August. It said then that it had passed a $7bn revenue run rate, the Journal reported.

Ghodsi told All-In the business is growing 70% to 80% a year, and that he wants more. He would like growth of 100%, “way over 100”, he noted.

According to recent reports, the manufacturer has added AI products this year. They include an agent called Genie One, which answers questions from a company’s business data, and Unity Gateway, which gives customers access to models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google. Genie also reached Slack this month, in a version that answers data questions inside the chat app.

Its investors include Coatue Management, Andreessen Horowitz, NEA, Insight Partners, Fidelity and J.P. Morgan Asset Management, according to the Journal.

The Journal calls Databricks a candidate for a near-term listing. Investors say a strong showing by Anthropic, whose stock ended its first week worth $1.6tn, could unleash a wave of AI listings, the paper reported.

In a recent development, ghodsi weighed both sides on All-In. A listing brings access to capital and a quarterly marketing moment, he mentioned. Staying private means Databricks must arrange its own tender offers for staff every year. He said employees have never used up all the money set aside for them.

It has been revealed that his worry is volatility. When a stock falls, he noted, staff effectively take a pay cut, and some leave before the price recovers.

In a recent development, ghodsi also remarked he believes AI has already reached artificial general intelligence, by the definition his Berkeley lab used around 2010. He put the risk of AI wiping out humanity close to zero, and named cyberattacks as the real danger.

“I think the cyber risk is real and really bad things can happen with that,” he remarked.
