Sierra raises $1.75B at a $30B valuation for AI customer service agents

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sierra, the AI agent manufacturer co-founded by OpenAI chair Bret Taylor, is raising $1.75B at a $30B valuation, led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed. That is almost double the $15.8B it was valued at in May, and it claims annual recurring revenue has passed $400M.

It has been revealed that sierra, the AI customer service company led by Bret Taylor, is raising $1.75B at a $30B valuation, it confirmed on Monday. Greenoaks and Lightspeed Venture Partners are leading the round.

Industry observers note that the valuation is almost double the $15.8B Sierra was worth in May, when it raised $950M. All of its previous lead investors, including Benchmark, Sequoia and Tiger Global, are investing again.

Sierra builds AI agents that answer customers’ calls and messages for large companies. It says its annual recurring revenue passed $400M in October, just over two and a half years after launch. Almost half of the Fortune 50 are customers, it says.

Sierra charges for outcomes, such as a resolved problem or a paid insurance claim, rather than per seat or per token, and Zendesk is moving the same way. Insurer Guardian recently handed it 1 million dental calls a year, and its agents can now take card payments through Stripe.

Taylor, who also chairs OpenAI’s board, co-founded Sierra with former Google executive Clay Bavor. In a blog post, the pair addressed fears of an AI bubble.

It has been revealed that “We’re seeing some of the oldest, most regulated companies in the world deploy AI the fastest,” they wrote. They pointed to last week’s record Anthropic IPO as a sign of how fast AI is moving.

Sources indicate that competition in AI customer service is heating up. In June, Salesforce agreed to buy support-AI firm Fin for $3.6B.
