It has been revealed that this July, as the Railroad Fire spread through Washington overnight, Watch Duty sent its first-ever nighttime Critical Alert to its users. It started with a 911 dispatch buried in radio static — and a newly introduced Gemini tool that caught it in seconds.

According to recent reports, A dedicated team of Google engineers and researchers spent six months embedded full-time with Watch Duty, a nonprofit wildfire alert app run by active and retired firefighters, dispatchers, and first responders. Through the Google.org Fellowship, they used more than 20,000 hours of past radio recordings to build a Gemini-powered tool that wipes out heavy static and translates specialized firefighting terminology into clean, searchable text. The system removes the added strain of deciphering noisy audio so volunteers can focus on verifying incidents and getting alerts out.

Recent findings suggest that when a wildfire starts, minutes can decide whether people get out safely. Yet across much of the country, the first word of a new fire still travels over decades-old analog VHF radio, where engine noise, wind, static, and rapid-fire tactical jargon make it hard to hear what's happening. Watch Duty reporters monitor up to 20 feeds manually, making it a challenge to cut through the noise.

As a result of the collaboration between Google and Watch Duty, volunteer reporter satisfaction with radio transcription tools increased from 35.3% to 92.3%.

The tool was also put to test during a live fire. In July 2026, a fast-moving wildfire — the Railroad Fire — ignited at night in Washington. When the first 911 dispatch went out over local fire radio, the Gemini-powered transcription tool transcribed it straight from the noisy radio audio and flagged it in under 30 seconds.

That head start gave Watch Duty's team the time they needed to verify the fire and push a Critical Alert—which breaks through do-not-disturb mode in urgent situations—helping residents evacuate before flames reached homes. It was the first time Watch Duty had ever sent a Critical Alert overnight, proving what faster detection means when communities are asleep and every minute counts.

The newly introduced AI radio tool is the latest milestone in a deep, multi-year collaboration spanning Google's philanthropic, product, and research teams to help Watch Duty scale its life-saving mission across all 50 states.

When the next fire starts, the goal is simple: get people the warning they need while there's still time to act.
