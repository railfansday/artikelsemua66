OpenAI releases a latest image classifier and ChatGPT warnings to combat CSAM

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

OpenAI today announced new tools to combat child sexual abuse material (CSAM) amid growing safety concerns around the use of AI tools. These features include a new image classifier to detect CSAM, warnings and temporary blocks to prevent consumers from creating or accessing content that sexualizes children, a new guideline for developers, and a set of tools for law enforcement.

The firm unveiled a newly introduced image classifier that can detect CSAM images, including the ones generated by AI. OpenAI is giving select companies access to its classifier through its API at no cost. The tool looks for visual signs of abuse in images not seen before and assigns a score that can give an indicative direction to reviewers about dealing with CSAM.

Sources indicate that openAI stated that it developed this classifier with the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC), and it is working with Tech Coalition and ROOST to share access to the tool. In its early testing with partners, the AI company found that the classifier flags 48% fewer images while finding the same number of abusive images, reducing the work of reviewers. Plus, images flagged by the classifier are twice as likely to be abusive images.

What’s more, the organization is adding upcoming warning screens in ChatGPT to prevent users from accessing material that sexualizes children. ChatGPT will show an alert to users attempting such prompts, direct users toward specialized support to stop harmful behavior, and also suspend the account for 24 hours as a warning. The app already blocks CSAM content and requests related to it. It said that repeated offences could lead to the account being removed permanently.

OpenAI noted that it formed the message and guidelines around this behavior with organizations and researchers, including Protect Children, the Lucy Faithfull Foundation, and Joel Scanlan at the University of Tasmania, who independently evaluated a trial of deterrent messages.

The tech giant is also publishing guidelines for developers using OpenAI tools to prevent, detect, and help users report CSAM content. It mentioned that in early testing, the guidelines have helped a number of developers adopt child safety tooling and register to report CSAM to NCMEC. OpenAI is also equipping law enforcement dealing with child safety with AI-powered features like dedicated plug-ins, investigative tools, and complex data analysis, with access managed by program administrators. It is also providing training to investigators along with a field guide on how to use AI.

It has been revealed that openAI has had several lawsuits against the manufacturer blaming its technology for harming people, especially children. Safety-focused nonprofit Common Sense Media labeled OpenAI’s tech as “unacceptable risk” for teens. Other AI chatbots — specifically Grok — have been used to create nonconsensual sexualized imagery, including of children.

The AI startup had made changes in the last 12 months to its technology, including releasing a safety blueprint, updating content guidelines to bar inappropriate content, retuning its model to prevent them from generating harmful content, and releasing a special version of ChatGPT for teens.
