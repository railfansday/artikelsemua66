It has been revealed that to mark the 100th anniversary of Colonial Williamsburg, we are adding thousands of digitized records and immersive stories to Google Arts & Culture. You can now explore rare manuscripts, view high-resolution archival photos, and watch a special culinary episode to learn about the history of early America. Visit our online hub to browse these collections and discover the stories behind these preserved treasures.

Mia Nagawiecki and Chance Coughenour are celebrating 100 years of Colonial Williamsburg by bringing its history to your screen. You can now explore thousands of newly digitized manuscripts, rare archival photos, and interactive galleries from home. These collections highlight the lives of ordinary people and the work behind preserving early American history. You can visit the Google Arts & Culture website to view these treasures, read latest stories, and learn about 18th-century traditions.

Today, as Colonial Williamsburg marks its 100th anniversary, Google Arts & Culture is sharing newly introduced immersive stories and newly digitized cultural artifacts that honor a century of preservation, discovery, and public access. These additions expand on the digital hub we introduced earlier this year with The Colonial Williamsburg Foundation to celebrate America’s 250th birthday and bring the rich history of early America online.

We’re introducing three new ways to explore Colonial Williamsburg’s legendary collections, taking you behind the scenes of how history is preserved, studied, and brought to life.

Explore newly digitized plates from naturalist Mark Catesby’s 18th-century masterwork, bringing 220 hand-engraved illustrations of North American wildlife and flora into brilliant, high resolution.

Step inside the newly introduced online gallery to see the restoration of Colonial Williamsburg, spotlighting its first three reconstructed sites: the Governor's Palace, the Raleigh Tavern, and the Capitol.

Dive into vivid watercolor and ink pages from folk artist Lewis Miller’s 19th-century sketchbook, offering a rare and powerful window into everyday Virginia life and the domestic slave trade.

Discover historic American transportation through a rare folio of carriage drawings, showcasing the visual diversity of Colonial Williamsburg's library collections.

Uncover the story of “Authentic Colors from Colonial Williamsburg” — a rare book featuring metal pages coated in historic automotive paints inspired by Colonial Williamsburg — and its unique conservation journey.

It has been revealed that in partnership with Colonial Williamsburg and YouTube, culinary historian Max Miller, creator of “Tasting History,” visited the living history museum to film an exclusive episode, bringing 18th-century traditions to life. Praising the museum’s meticulous historical accuracy, Miller worked alongside Ivey Boyd, chef de cuisine at the King’s Arms Tavern, to prepare snow eggs (oeufs à la neige). This French dessert was popularized in early America by James Hemings, Thomas Jefferson's enslaved chef, and is served today at Colonial Williamsburg. This collaboration bridges traditional preservation with modern storytelling, inviting a new generation to discover the flavors and stories of early America.

Sources indicate that by expanding access to Colonial Williamsburg’s collections, we’re safeguarding centuries-old treasures while sharing their history with the world. As Colonial Williamsburg enters its second century, we invite you to experience these new stories, archival records, and the living history of early America in a whole new way.
