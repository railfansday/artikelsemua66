Suspected China spy linked to Nvidia's ex-boss left in 2025, Megaspeed says

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Nvidia scandal has sent shockwaves through Silicon Valley as well as the political establishment.

Alice Huang, a suspected China spy who has been linked to Nvidia's former boss Jensen Huang, left her executive role at Megaspeed in August last year, the firm states.

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of the AI chip-making giant Nvidia after it emerged he had allegedly had affairs with Alice Huang as well as with one of his employees.

Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in South East Asia and the scandal at Nvidia has raised questions about the possibility that high-end AI chips may have ended up in China despite US export restrictions.

The Singapore-based manufacturer stated on Sunday it had rolled out an internal investigation into its business.

Megaspeed mentioned it was aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

It added that it "conducts legitimate and lawful business" and has "retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation".

Alice Huang stepped down from her role as director of Megaspeed near the end of 2023, according to Washington-based security analysis group C4ADS but it remarked she was still "practically in control" of the company.

Alice Huang - who is of no relation to Nvidia's former boss - had been investigated by Singapore authorities in February 2025 as part of the country's probe into Megaspeed and other companies.

Also on Sunday, US Congressman John Moolenaar remarked that Jensen Huang had agreed to testify before the House Select Committee on China, which he chairs, as well as the Foreign Affairs and the Intelligence committees.

The committees had threatened to concern subpoenas to Jensen Huang if he did not comply with their requests to testify.

Moolenaar told CBS, BBC's US partner, that none were ultimately issued.

Jensen Huang's testimony is expected to take place behind close doors, with the dates still being finalised.

Moolenaar said: "That's going to be a very important meeting to understand to what degree privileged information was accessed, and to what degree this potential alleged Chinese spy influenced Nvidia's philosophy and may have had an influence on some of our policies with respect to AI and export controls.".

Separately, a group of tech leaders including Space X's Elon Musk, have been trying to rally support to have Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive.

A representative forJensen Huang declined to comment on a report in the Financial Times suggesting that he was discussing a comeback to the firm he co-founded.

The BBC understands that the likelihood of him being reinstated is very low while the company's internal investigations are ongoing.

The move would also face scrutiny from Washington, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent calling for Nvidia to hire its next chief executive from outside the manufacturer.

For the moment, Nvidia's chief financial officer Colette Kress is leading the firm and will present the organization's results on Tuesday, 17 November.

On Friday, Bessent blamed the US Commerce Department, led by fellow cabinet colleague Howard Lutnick, for failing to properly oversee the export of high-end chips.

Sources indicate that bessent told CNBC that several agencies "were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... but Commerce has the pen... and you will have to ask them about their internal process".

The so-called entity list restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals.

Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips.

Other congressional committees have asked both Lutnick and his department to retain all records relating to Nvidia and Jensen Huang.

Megaspeed is suspected of giving backdoor access to Nvidia chips to Chinese companies via its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.

Nvidia has been banned from selling its advanced semiconductors to China.

While Singapore does not prohibit the transfer of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, such moves can breach US export controls.

Singapore has said that while it does not enforce "unilateral export controls imposed by other countries" it expects all companies in its jurisdiction to respect such regulations.

Megaspeed noted it "designs and maintains its global operational and supply-chain control frameworks to comply with all applicable laws and regulatory regimes". It said this includes "US export control requirements and local jurisdiction rules in every market where we operate".

China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy and warned the US not to use the scandal as a "pretext for imposing a technological blockade" on AI technology.
