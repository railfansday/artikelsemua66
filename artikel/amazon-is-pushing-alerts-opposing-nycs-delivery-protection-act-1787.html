Amazon claims that a new bill could add $5 per order for New Yorkers.

Amazon is serving up push notifications and banners on its home page to some customers, warning that a upcoming bill could add up to $5 per order in New York City. The Delivery Protection Act, which is backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani and a number of labor groups, including the Teamsters, would require Amazon to employ its current fleet of third-party delivery contractors directly.

At the moment, Amazon turns to a network of small delivery companies that often work exclusively with the retailer. This is why your packages are dropped off by a guy in a Toyota Camry sometimes, rather than an Amazon truck. The new bill would require that last-mile delivery firms be licensed by the city and directly employed, rather than operate as contractors.

Next to Black Friday deal offers, now Amazon is showing a big tile with a red banner that says, “Attention NYC Council could add up to $5 per order.” Other owners are reporting similar notices in the mobile app and after completing orders.

In a statement posted to its site, Amazon indicates that:.

Recent findings suggest that if this legislation passes, we’d no longer be able to work with them [third-party delivery contractors], and we’d be forced to consider moving our operations outside of the city. That would likely mean slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers in Newly introduced York City, while also threatening small businesses and their thousands of employees.

Industry observers note that mayor Mandani and labor unions have countered that the current subcontractor model allows corporations like Amazon to avoid accountability and exploit vulnerable workers. In a statement to The Verge City Council Speaker Julie Menin said:.

Industry observers note that “It defies logic that Amazon, a $3 trillion corporation, would raise Fresh Yorkers’ cost of living if it’s forced to adhere to basic regulations and worker protections. We are crafting legislation that regulates Amazon and protects workers, without impacting prices or jobs. If Amazon thinks this deceptive campaign will effectively threaten New Yorkers and intimidate this City Council, they are dead wrong.”.

Amazon’s threats to leave Newly introduced York to avoid regulation and efforts to unionize within the city follows shortly after the company’s third-quarter earnings report in which it noted that it was on its way to having not one, but two trillion-dollar businesses in the next five to ten years.
