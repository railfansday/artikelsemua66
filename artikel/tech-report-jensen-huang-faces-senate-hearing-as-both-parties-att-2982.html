Tech Report: Jensen Huang faces Senate hearing as both parties attack his return

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (740 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has called Jensen Huang to a public hearing at 10am on Thursday, 3 December, according to a notice.
  • Huang had agreed to testify before his return.
  • It has been revealed that lawmakers from both parties criticised the board’s decision on Monday.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Senate Banking Committee chair Tim Scott has called Jensen Huang to a public hearing at 10am on Thursday, 3 December, according to a notice. The hearing will come less than two weeks after Nvidia’s board reinstated him as chief executive, Politico reported.

Huang had agreed to testify before his return. A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment on whether his aims have changed, Politico’s Jasper Goodman reported.

It has been revealed that lawmakers from both parties criticised the board’s decision on Monday. Several mentioned it made it more urgent to pass tighter controls on chip exports to China.

Nvidia’s board stated in its announcement that it relied on input from senior levels of the US government. On that basis, it said Huang’s return poses no national security risk. Senator John Cornyn of Texas asked what that was based on.

Sources indicate that brian Mast, who chairs the House Foreign Affairs Committee, called the reports about Huang “extremely concerning” on X. In a video posted minutes after the announcement, he said Huang still owes Congress answers under oath. A committee spokesperson told Politico the committee still expects Huang’s closed-door interview with three House committees on 1 December to go ahead.

Sources indicate that “The American people deserve answers on Mr. Huang’s relationship with this potential Chinese intelligence asset,” Mast stated.

Representative Don Bacon of Nebraska stated Nvidia should have moved on without Huang, Reuters reported.

“Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy,” Bacon remarked.

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer remarked in a statement that the administration’s role in Huang’s return indicated Congress must act.

“Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other NVIDIA executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain,” Schumer stated.

It has been revealed that representative Raja Krishnamoorthi called the decision “outrageous” on X. He said Trump’s assurances could not be taken at face value, citing a $25m donation from Nvidia to Trump’s ballroom. Huang must testify at a full public hearing, he wrote.

Huang’s return is likely to affect his legal representation before Congress, Semafor reported. The administration might also push back on the oversight aims, it remarked.

Leaders in both parties are leaning towards using the coming defence bill to close gaps in chip export policy, Semafor reported. Mast is among those pushing to attach export control bills to the National Defense Authorization Act, according to Politico.

Trump told CNBC on Monday that he did not think the board would have brought Huang back without him. The White House did not respond to Semafor’s request for comment.
