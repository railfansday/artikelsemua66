The world's largest LGBTQ+ dating app, Grindr, has defended its $120 (Â£91) per month premium subscription service, which launches globally today.

Edge offers AI-powered services such as daily personalised profile recommendations that aim to provide better matches and limit scrolling.

The company's CEO, George Arison, told BBC News it would be a "very different Grindr experience" which was aimed at a "small community of owners" - and was unapologetic about its cost.

"I think we're a capitalist society, and everything hinges on whether you can afford it or not," he said.

Grindr has long had a free service, and offers two additional tiers that cost between $22.99 and $44.99 per month.

There was a mixed reaction from owners BBC News spoke to in San Francisco to the new, much more expensive version of the app.

"The current premium Grindr tier is already really expensive," remarked a user named Rybin, who told the BBC the firm's newest offering "just seems really out of touch with the community.".

"I think that that's really disheartening, and it kind of leaves a bad taste in my mouth," said another user, Adrian.

Both declined to give their last name.

"The cost doesn't bother me," mentioned a user named Michael. "All of these apps have premium tiers so saying that Grindr shouldn't have that when like it exists across all these other apps used for connection doesn't make sense.".

He noted that high-end dating apps like The League can charge much more.

Grindr has been experimenting with Edge for much of the last year in markets including the US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand.

On its earnings call earlier this month, the company said almost 400,000 customers had registered for the service in the first 72 hours that it was available for testing.

Inside Grindr, Edge is viewed as a way to drive future growth in an industry has struggled in recent years.

In a recent development, for those who can afford it, the service promises to offer insights about a user's compatibility with prospective matches. It also resurfaces a user's past connections alongside summaries of previous interactions.

According to recent reports, the current $120 monthly fee comes after the company tested charging hundreds of dollars more than that. And Arison has left open the possibility of increasing the cost in the future.

Arison told the BBC he's not concerned that intoducing an ultra-premium offering will pit "haves" against "have-nots" on the social networking platform.

Edge users "being willing to pay more for that set of features will result in us being able to subsidise the product for everybody else," Arison mentioned.

"It's a choice that people make on whether they want to be on it.".

The use of AI to develop the Edge service is part of Grindr's attempt to become what it calls an "AI-first company.".

Arison said the manufacturer was investing in AI to ensure it can build products more promptly and cheaply.

In September, as the debate over AI safety raged throughout Silicon Valley, Arison told the BBC that his concerns about what he called an "anti-civilisational worldview at Anthropic" had prompted him to instruct some of his engineers to stop using Anthropic's technology.

Anthropic is due to start selling shares to the public for the first time this week.

Although Anthropic's Claude Code tools remain in use at Grindr now, Arison noted the organization is on track to completely phase it out of his company's workflows by next year.

He said Grindr was once a very heavy user of Claude Code but that it was no longer the primary tool the use to write code.

In a recent development, "Anthropic's built an amazing amazing set of products, and they should be very proud of what they've done," Arison noted. "They should aim to win with the awesome products they have, rather than regulatory capture that they're trying to win with, oftentimes.".
