It has given Meta, which also runs WhatsApp and Instagram, 60 days to disable the feature by default and seek users' consent separately.

The fine is among the largest an Italian authority has ever imposed on a tech tech giant.

A Meta spokesperson told the BBC that it planned to appeal.

The charge is related to a feature that suggests photos and videos from a user's phone gallery as ideas for posts.

Industry observers note that facebook requested access to owners' photo galleries through a single app-wide authorisation. A preceding notice did not make clear that this would also allow Facebook to generate suggestions based on photos in their gallery, according to the Italian regulator.

The regulator stated it had taken into account "the high number of people affected" and the fact that the feature was "still ongoing for those who have not updated the app and for those who have already given their consent to the system".

It also cited "Meta's lack of co-operation" in its decision.

Meta told the BBC: "We strongly disagree with the [regulator's] assessment of the facts, its application of the law and the disproportionate level of the fine.
