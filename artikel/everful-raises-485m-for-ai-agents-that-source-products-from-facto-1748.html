📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that san Francisco startup Everful has raised a $48.5M Series A led by Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India and January Capital. Its AI agents find and vet factories and manage orders for retailers and brands, and it says it is on track for $100M in annualised revenue by year-end.

According to recent reports, everful, a San Francisco startup that uses AI agents to source products from factories, has raised a $48.5M Series A, the company confirmed on Thursday. Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India and January Capital led the round, with existing investors Square Peg and Lightspeed taking part.

Retailers and brands usually buy stock through layers of middlemen, long email threads and high minimum orders. Everful’s agents spot trending products, find and vet factories, and handle the messages for each order.

Everful scores each factory on quality using its own inspections, then ships the goods itself. Customers can buy wholesale, dropship or order custom products with low minimums. Quality checks are drawing AI investment too, with Smartex launching AI fabric checks for H&M on Thursday.

“AI lets us turn that team into software,” stated co-founder and CEO Sisi Song, referring to the overseas sourcing teams big retailers hire.

It has been revealed that founded in 2024, Everful indicates it now ships more than 5 million items a month to customers in over 60 countries. It works with more than 30,000 factories and says it is on track for $100M in annualised revenue by year-end. Those figures come from the firm and have not been independently confirmed.

According to recent reports, song is a former Alibaba executive and Bessemer Venture Partners investor. Co-founder Kevin Han previously sold his AI startup, Orbeus, to Amazon.

The money will fund growth in the US, its biggest market, and Europe. Investors are betting on AI agents across the supply chain: Freehand raised $75M in July to manage company spending, and Plato raised $14.5M to automate wholesale trade.

According to recent reports, cheap cross-border shopping is also under pressure. Shein, which lost its US tariff exemption, is pitching its IPO at under $30B, far below its 2022 valuation.
