Tech Report: Genesis Introduces Hybrids at the LA Auto Show, EREV Coming in 2027

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Korean luxury automaker Genesis debuted its first hybrid models this week, debuting fresh powertrains for the G80 sedan and GV80 SUV at the 2026 LA Auto Show. The manufacturer says the electrified system is both more efficient and more powerful than the current nonhybrid versions. For buyers who want a more battery-focused experience, Genesis teased an upcoming 640-mile extended-range electric vehicle due to arrive in early 2027.

The hybridized versions of the brand’s most popular sedan and SUV arrive as American drivers show growing interest in energy-efficient cars, both battery electrics and hybrids, driven by high fuel costs and better understanding of the technologies. Hybrids, in particular, have attracted attention because they behave like traditional combustion cars: There’s no need to install home charging hardware or change familiar driving and refueling habits. Just get in and reap better fuel economy with every mile.

Sources indicate that the G80 Hybrid and GV80 Hybrid share a 2.5-liter turbocharged hybrid powertrain. Genesis claims the system is 16% more powerful than the gasoline-only version, outputting 347 horsepower and 391 pound-feet of torque. A latest liquid-cooled battery pack improves thermal management and allows higher maximum discharge than Hyundai Motor Group’s previous air-cooled hybrid batteries, boosting performance. Both models use a seven-speed automatic transmission adapted to work with the electric motor’s regenerative braking.

According to recent reports, genesis hasn’t unveiled official fuel economy estimates, but notes the G80 Hybrid is 30% more efficient than the standard model, while the GV80 Hybrid is 24% more efficient. Based on current EPA figures, my math puts them in the ballpark of 30 mpg and 27 mpg, respectively. Any improvement is good, but those aren’t exactly earth-shattering gains. We’ll have to wait for the official ratings to be sure.

Recent findings suggest that each hybrid can be toggled into a silent EV mode for short distances — good for gliding into a driveway, garage or parking deck without waking the neighbors. However, without a plug, all the energy in the battery ultimately comes from the gas tank.

A new Stay Mode lets owners run the climate control and infotainment without starting the engine for around 30 minutes. I like to think of it as the “moment of zen in the driveway before you have to go inside and face responsibility” mode, or maybe the “eating lunch in your car” mode. It’s similar to Toyota’s My Room Mode on its hybrids.

Aside from the powertrain, the G80 and GV80 Hybrids are largely identical to their combustion counterparts. Hybrid-specific touches include a three-dimensional G-Matrix grille inlay redesigned for these models, as well as newly introduced exterior colors.

It has been revealed that inside, a 27-inch OLED display combining the instrument cluster and navigation stretches halfway across the dashboard. Tech features include Digital Key 2 Premium, which lets owners use their phone instead of a key fob to unlock and start the car, and active road noise cancellation. Genesis’ driver-assistance suite is headlined by the Highway Drive Assist 2 steering assistance system and the Remote Smart Parking Assist 2, which enables automatic parking with the driver inside or just nearby the car.

In a recent development, prices for the sedan and SUV haven’t been declared. Final specifications and EPA fuel economy figures are expected closer to the early 2027 launch window.

According to recent reports, my cynical side says that these new models are just Genesis doing what Toyota has done since the RX 400h debuted in 2004. (To be fair, Genesis didn’t become an independent brand until 2015, so its rival has a bit of a head start.) In practical terms, the move brings Hyundai Motor Group’s luxury brand into parity with other group members and luxury car competitors that have rolled out updated hybrids over the last year, and meets customers’ increasing demand for more energy-efficient cars where they are.

In addition to its first hybrid sedan and SUV, Genesis executives announced that the brand will expand the availability of its wild-styled, high-performance GV60 Magma full-electric SUV to more states and markets to meet higher-than-expected demand.

In a recent development, at the debut event in LA, the brand also teased an upcoming extended-range EV — an EV with an onboard gasoline generator, essentially a very advanced plug-in hybrid — set to launch in early 2027. The EREV model is targeting over 640 miles before its “all-fresh high-performance battery” and gas tank both run dry, but Genesis didn’t give further details about the upcoming model.
