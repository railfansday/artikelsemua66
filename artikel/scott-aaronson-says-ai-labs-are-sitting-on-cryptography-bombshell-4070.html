Scott Aaronson says AI labs are sitting on cryptography ‘bombshells’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Computer scientist Scott Aaronson indicates he is “virtually certain” that leading AI labs are holding back results that would break some of today’s cryptography. He made the claim in a post on his blog, Shtetl-Optimized, on Wednesday. He also warned that he does not know what those results actually are.

“Because people have been hounding me about it: yes, I’m virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells in that area,” Aaronson wrote.

Recent findings suggest that until he sees details or hears from someone at a lab, he feels “much, much less confident” about what exactly they are. TNW has not independently verified the claims.

Industry observers note that the post followed a comment Aaronson left on his blog on Tuesday. In it, he said he had heard “many, many rumors” that one or both labs had a fast way to factor large numbers. That would break RSA, the encryption behind much of the web’s security. The comment spread widely on X, and he later added a note to it.

“A rumor is a rumor is a rumor, and the parts that I’m ‘virtually certain’ about fall well short of Factoring in P,” he wrote.

In a recent development, google published a paper on Wednesday with a newly introduced factoring algorithm. Aaronson mentioned it held major theoretical results, but nothing of practical use for breaking encryption.

In a recent development, the rumours come during a run of maths results from OpenAI and Anthropic. On Tuesday, both labs published proofs of VP ≠ VNP. The problem has long been seen as a step towards P ≠ NP, the most famous open question in computer science.

According to recent reports, openAI also posted a paper claiming that the exponent of matrix multiplication is exactly 9/4. The 111-page paper indicates multiplying two n×n matrices takes at least n9/4/2 multiplication or division steps. The question dates back to Volker Strassen’s work in the late 1960s.

In a recent development, last week, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge noted he and Claude had proved the Riemann hypothesis. The Lean proof assistant has since certified the proof. In October, OpenAI said its model had disproved a famous hypothesis.

“I now feel in my bones, for the first time ever, that a proof of P ≠ NP is not far,” Aaronson wrote.

Sources indicate that he asked readers not to let cryptography overshadow the maths. If their main worry was a Bitcoin wallet, he wrote, they should look for rumours elsewhere. He also urged people to accept that the AI proofs are real, now that labs back them with Lean certificates.
