As Gemini becomes more capable, we continually advance our security defenses. When you assign Gemini a task, it uses Google Cloud’s Agent Compute Engine to work in a hardware-isolated virtual machine that’s dedicated to your account and never shared with other people. Each virtual machine is sandboxed, so Gemini works in a separate, isolated environment that’s regularly tested by our security red teams. This sandbox is just one of multiple layers of defense, including prompt-injection filters that scan search results, webpages, and more alongside Google Safe Browsing to limit which links Gemini can open.

Sources indicate that as Gemini works on a task, you can jump in to give it more instructions at any point, and it’s also designed to pause and check with you before critical steps. For example, Gemini is designed to ask for your confirmation or pre-approval before clicking "Buy," and will ask clarifying questions if an instruction is ambiguous. For routine stuff you already trust, like adding those Cheerios to your grocery cart, you can set that to “Always allow” so it doesn’t pester you.

And finally, we give you access to settings and tools that help you safeguard your data and protect your privacy. You can adjust task settings, disconnect Google and third-party apps, or delete chat history anytime. For a closer look at how we’ve implemented a multi-layer defense-in-depth security strategy, read our technical paper.

And please participate in our bug bounty program through Google’s AI Vulnerability Reward Program (AI VRP) to advance the security of agentic systems.
