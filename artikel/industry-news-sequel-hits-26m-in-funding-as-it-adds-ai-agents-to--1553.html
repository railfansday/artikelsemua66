Sources indicate that sequel has raised $15M led by Sorenson Capital, taking its total to $26M. Its software runs webinars on a company’s own site and links what viewers do there, and it now sells AI agents that proposal video programmes and turn talks into other content.

According to recent reports, sequel, a startup that runs webinars on a company’s own website rather than on a separate platform, has raised $15M, co-founder and CEO Oana Manolache wrote on Wednesday. Sorenson Capital led the round, with High Alpha, Album VC and Webflow Ventures joining. It takes Sequel’s total funding to $26M.

Manolache, who previously worked in marketing at HP, notes standalone webinar tools send interested viewers away from a organization’s site. Sequel keeps them there, and links what they watched and asked to what they did before and after.

Recent findings suggest that it now sells AI agents built on that data. They can blueprint a video programme, turn one talk into other content and help turn viewers into sales leads. Customers can also plug in their own AI tools through an MCP server.

“Anyone can build an agent now. I can build one in five minutes in my spare time. But an agent without context is useless,” Manolache wrote.

Sequel says it has tripled revenue two years in a row and serves more than 400 companies, including ElevenLabs and Datadog. It did not share revenue figures or a valuation. It last raised an $8M Series A in 2022.

Sources indicate that its pitch leans on trust, as AI makes fakes easier to produce. Zoom has started letting customers prove they are human on calls. Marketing tools are filling up with agents too: Adobe this month put its marketing agent inside ChatGPT and Claude, and Gradial raised $65M in June.
