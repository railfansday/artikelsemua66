One of the many benefits of the newly introduced Siri AI is its ability to work directly with apps on your device via a feature known as App Intents. Currently, this skill includes many of Apple’s own built-in apps and some third-party ones. But with the latest public and developer betas for iOS 27.3, the company is expanding support to all third-party developers.

In a recent development, with App Intents, you’re able to ask Siri to tackle a variety of actions among various built-in and third-party apps. On a basic level, you can tell it to perform tasks such as getting directions, playing music, scheduling meetings, sending messages, adding reminders, and finding photos.

On a more advanced level, you can ask it to do things like organize your inbox, edit a photo, and analyze and help you with your current screen. You could also tell it to combine several actions in one shot, work across multiple apps, and engage in a natural back-and-forth conversation.

Introduced in 2022 with iOS 16, the App Intents skill does play well with classic Siri, no Apple Intelligence required. This applies not just to built-in apps but also to third-party apps. However, classic Siri lacks the AI smarts and advanced skills of the latest version.

Siri AI fully supports most built-in apps, including Calendar, Files, Finder, Journal, Mail, Maps, Messages, Music, Notes, Photos, Preview, Reminders, Safari, Shortcuts, and Weather. It does support some third-party apps, but its more advanced features are limited or unavailable.

That’s the gap the fresh developer betas aim to close. With iOS 27.3, iPadOS 27.3, MacOS 27.3, and VisionOS 27.3, all developers will be able to use Siri AI to fully support and integrate with their apps. For example, instead of manually opening an app like DoorDash or Grubhub to check or change a food order, you can tell Siri to do it for you.

Recent findings suggest that “Today’s preview allows any app to bring its content and actions directly into Siri AI by using App Intents, making it easier for all apps to tap into Siri AI’s newest capabilities, including personal context understanding, on-screen awareness, natural language, and more,” Apple remarked in a news release for developers. “So people can just ask for help in their own words, and Siri AI carries out any custom action you’ve built, from checking a delivery to booking a reservation to placing an order.”.

As I use many third-party apps on my iPhone and iPad, both personally and professionally, I’m looking forward to enhanced support for the ones I rely on. With that in mind, here are five apps for which I’d welcome the latest and full Siri AI integration.

Industry observers note that I often use Amazon on my iPhone and iPad to shop for and purchase items, review my wishlist, and check the status of products I’ve ordered. To do any of those things, I have to open the app and manually go to the right screens. How convenient it would be if I could tell Siri to handle those tasks for me.

“Siri, search for the 512GB 2026 iPad mini on Amazon and add it to my shopping cart.”.

“Siri, review my Amazon wishlist for the luggage I recently added and tell me the latest prices.”.

“Siri, check the delivery status of the Alexa photo frame I just ordered on Amazon.”.

In a recent development, right now, Siri can’t do any of those things. But if Siri AI and App intents are fully integrated with Amazon, I may just be able to accomplish those tasks in the future.

IMDB is my go-to app for getting info on movies, TV demonstrates, performers, directors, and more. After I open the app and run a search, I often want specific details, such as the rating and runtime of a particular film or TV episode.

I can then borrow a book and read it through the Libby app or, more typically, through the Amazon Kindle app. For this, I have to manually open the app and search among the various libraries for the book I want.
