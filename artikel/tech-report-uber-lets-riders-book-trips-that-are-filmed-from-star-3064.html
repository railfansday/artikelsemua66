Tech Report: Uber lets riders book trips that are filmed from start to finish

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (341 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Uber is piloting Recorded Rides in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St.
  • Recent findings suggest that uber is testing a ride option in which the whole trip is filmed, TechCrunch reported on Monday.
  • The pilot is starting in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Uber is piloting Recorded Rides in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St. Louis, letting riders book drivers who have turned on video recording for the whole trip. The footage stays encrypted on the driver’s phone and is deleted after 14 days unless it is attached to a safety report.

Recent findings suggest that uber is testing a ride option in which the whole trip is filmed, TechCrunch reported on Monday. “Recorded Rides” matches riders with drivers who have switched on Uber’s Record My Ride feature, which uses the front camera of the driver’s phone.

The pilot is starting in Baltimore, Raleigh, Indianapolis and St. Louis, with more cities to follow. Uber stated it is aimed at late-night trips and rides booked for teens.

Industry observers note that uber is also testing recording that switches on by itself when a car goes off its route, or when the app detects a possible crash. Drivers can opt out in their settings, and riders can decline through an in-app notice, according to TechCrunch. Cameras can protect drivers too: 22 Uber drivers were among more than 50 US gig workers killed on the job over five years, a 2022 study found.

In a recent development, under Uber’s rules for Record My Ride, the video is encrypted and stored only on the driver’s phone, according to Uber’s help page. Recordings are deleted after 14 days unless they are sent in with a safety report or insurance claim. Uber says it can share them with police under legal process or in an emergency.

Uber first tested recording trip audio in 2019, and has lately added cameras for younger riders. In August, it let parents watch their teen’s ride live on video through the driver’s phone, though other passengers could appear on the stream. Code in its app also hints at rides for children aged 10 to 12.
