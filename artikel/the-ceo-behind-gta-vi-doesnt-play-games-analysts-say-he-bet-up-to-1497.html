Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick does not play the games his company sells, Fortune reported, as GTA VI launches with an estimated $1B to $1.5B budget. Take-Two has not confirmed the cost, but the game has tied Metacritic’s record score and pre-orders beat expectations.

It has been revealed that strauss Zelnick has run Take-Two Interactive, which owns GTA studio Rockstar, since 2011. He does not play the games it sells, Fortune reported in a profile republished on Wednesday, the eve of Grand Theft Auto VI’s launch.

“I don’t think being consumer-in-chief really helps a CEO be effective in this business,” Zelnick told Business Insider.

Industry observers note that analysts cited by Business Insider estimate GTA VI cost $1B to $1.5B to make. Take-Two has not confirmed a figure, and Zelnick has only said the game “was expensive”.

In a recent development, the bet looks set to pay off. GTA VI tied Metacritic’s all-time record with a score of 99, and Take-Two noted pre-orders beat expectations. Sensor Tower data puts pre-orders at 17 million, according to Fortune.

Industry observers note that the game costs from $80 and comes without a physical disc, as Sony plans to stop making discs for newly introduced games from 2028. Its predecessor, GTA V, has sold more than 215 million copies since 2013.

In a recent development, under Zelnick, Take-Two’s shares have risen more than 1,700%, and its revenue grew roughly fivefold to $5.6B in 2025. He has also mentioned generative AI played no part in making GTA VI, which Rockstar built by hand.
