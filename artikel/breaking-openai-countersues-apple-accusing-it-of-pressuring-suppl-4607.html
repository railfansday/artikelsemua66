Breaking: OpenAI countersues Apple, accusing it of pressuring suppliers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

OpenAI has filed counterclaims against Apple, accusing it of pressuring suppliers not to work on OpenAI’s devices and using broad employment agreements to stop engineers joining OpenAI, Bloomberg reported. Apple sued OpenAI in July, alleging theft of hardware trade secrets.

In a recent development, openAI states Apple has pushed suppliers not to work on its devices, Bloomberg reported. It made the claims in a court filing in San Jose on Friday, hitting back at Apple’s trade-secrets lawsuit.

“Apple has responded to OpenAI’s anticipated entry into the hardware market by unlawfully pressuring vendors not to work with OpenAI,” the manufacturer said in the filing.

Sources indicate that according to OpenAI, Apple asks suppliers to name their OpenAI projects and the staff on them who once worked for Apple. It then tries to keep anyone who has worked on OpenAI products off Apple jobs. That makes suppliers wary of putting staff on OpenAI work.

One supplier moved its OpenAI work to the night shift so it would not overlap with Apple work, which raised OpenAI’s costs, the filing states. Others have moved meetings with OpenAI so Apple would not see them.

OpenAI also takes aim at the contracts Apple staff sign. It indicates they treat almost everything workers learn as secret and stop them hiring away Apple staff for a year. It repeats its claim that a former worker who returned to Apple took more than 100 files about an unreleased OpenAI device.

Apple did not immediately respond to Bloomberg. It has stated before that it found no evidence for OpenAI’s claims.

Apple sued in July. It notes OpenAI and its hardware chief Tang Tan used former Apple staff to get its secrets, and that OpenAI had a shared supplier copy an Apple metal-finishing method. In October, OpenAI agreed not to use the files Apple says were taken while the case goes on.

The fight hangs over OpenAI’s aims to sell devices designed with Jony Ive, starting with a doughnut-shaped speaker. The two once worked together to bring ChatGPT to Siri, a partnership that has since turned sour.
