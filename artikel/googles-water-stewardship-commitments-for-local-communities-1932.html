Data centers are the nerve centers of the digital world, powering our daily lives with Search, Maps, online banking, healthcare and 911 systems. As we grow our data center footprint to support these services, we recognize that how we build is just as crucial as what we build.

Water is a critical component of data center development and operations. Because data centers generate heat from the servers and chips that power the digital world, they often rely on water to cut back on energy use for cooling. In many places, water cooling can reduce data center energy use by approximately 10% compared to air cooling. The aggregate water consumption of data centers is small — U.S. data centers use less than 1% of the water that Americans use on their lawns annually 1 — but we are focused on protecting local water resources in all aspects of our data center operations.

We are committed to responsibly manage vital water resources where we build and operate data centers. Our goal is to minimize our local impacts so that our growth does not come at the expense of the communities we call home. We’re also announcing newly introduced water stewardship initiatives and evaluating projects through Google’s water replenishment Request for Information (RFI).

To further our replenishment goals, we’re also evaluating more than 700 projects submitted through our recent Water Replenishment Projects RFI. These projects include engineered solutions to improve water efficiency, supply or quality; farm-level practices to reduce agricultural water demand and improve soil health; and nature-based solutions aimed at improving the hydrologic cycle. We’ll have more to share in the coming months as we work with partners to roll out these projects.

Water is an essential natural resource that communities work hard to conserve. Through our water stewardship commitments, we will continue to be responsible and transparent in our water use — protecting the long-term health of the watersheds in communities we call home.
