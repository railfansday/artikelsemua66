Baidu’s AI cloud revenue nearly doubles, but ads pull sales down again

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Baidu Building, the tech giant’s headquarters in Beijing.

Recent findings suggest that baidu’s AI cloud infrastructure business nearly doubled in the third quarter. Its revenue rose 94% from a year earlier to RMB8.2bn. It still could not lift the group. Total revenue fell 1% to RMB30.8bn ($4.60bn) as Baidu’s search advertising kept shrinking.

The figures come from Baidu’s third-quarter results, published on Monday. Bloomberg reported that it was Baidu’s sixth straight quarterly revenue drop. The total was in line with the average analyst forecast.

According to recent reports, revenue from online marketing services fell 22% to RMB11.9bn. Ads now make up 48% of Baidu General Business, down from 62% a year earlier. That unit holds search, cloud and apps. It grew 1% to RMB25.0bn. Streaming service iQIYI, which Baidu reports on its own line, fell 10% to RMB6.0bn.

Recent findings suggest that baidu groups its AI products into one line, Baidu Core AI-powered Business. It brought in RMB13.2bn, up 37%, and made up 53% of General Business revenue, against 39% a year earlier. Baidu notes that these figures come from unaudited internal management accounts.

In a recent development, most of the growth came from Baidu AI Cloud. Revenue from GPU Cloud, which rents out AI computing, rose 191% from a year earlier and 43% on the previous quarter. Revenue from outside customers’ token usage on Qianfan, Baidu’s model platform, rose 1,159%.

Recent findings suggest that the rest of the AI line was flat or worse. AI Applications held at RMB2.6bn. AI-native marketing fell 15% to RMB2.4bn. Baidu blamed a tough economy and rising competition. It also cited its own choice to put user experience ahead of near-term revenue in AI Search.

“Our long-term investments in full-stack AI are translating into meaningful growth at scale,” stated Robin Li, Baidu’s co-founder and CEO.

In a recent development, net income attributable to Baidu was RMB1.4bn ($208m). A year earlier, Baidu reported a net loss of RMB11.2bn after an impairment charge on assets in its core business. Operating income was RMB626m, a 2% margin. Adjusted earnings were RMB6.78 ($1.01) per American depositary share.

It has been revealed that operating cash flow was RMB2.8bn. Baidu held RMB276.6bn in total cash and investments at the end of September. It has returned $373m to shareholders through buybacks since the start of 2026. Baidu expects to pay its first dividend before the end of 2026, subject to board approval.

“We will maintain a disciplined approach to capital allocation with a clear focus on investment returns as we continue to invest in AI,” remarked Haijian He, Baidu’s CFO.

This month Baidu unveiled ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview, the newest model in its family. Baidu says it ranks second among open-source models on the Code Arena WebDev leaderboard. The launch follows Baidu’s claim that its AI research agent sped up work on the model.

Bloomberg reported that the Ernie series has fallen out of the top tier and lags upstarts DeepSeek and Moonshot. Rivals Tencent and Alibaba have pulled ahead, it stated. Alibaba launched its Qwen4-Max model on Monday morning.

Sources indicate that baidu’s chip unit, Kunlunxin, expects its M100 inference chip to be ready for volume production by the end of 2026. The unit is preparing a Hong Kong listing. Bloomberg News has reported that the debut could slip to next year.

Baidu “lacks the scale to compete with China’s tech platforms”, Bloomberg Intelligence analysts Robert Lea and Jasmine Lyu wrote.

Apollo Go, Baidu’s robotaxi service, reached 30 cities as of October. Its fleets drove about 34 million autonomous kilometres in the quarter. Baidu doubled its fleet in Dubai, where Apollo runs on Uber. In London, it keeps testing on open roads with Uber and Lyft.

In the second quarter, ads made up 52% of General Business revenue and the AI line 50%. Baidu holds its earnings call at 7:30am ET on Monday.
