Latest Update: Did Christopher Nolan Just Bring 4K Blu-Ray Discs Back to Stores?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Physical media lovers, take note: Best Buy has begun selling 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray discs in-store and online. If you’ve been paying attention, you’ll know how big a pivot this is for the electronics retailer. Back in 2023, the manufacturer unveiled it would cease all sales of DVDs and Blu-rays entirely.

In a recent development, now, thanks to Christopher Nolan’s blockbuster phenom and Oscar frontrunner The Odyssey, which is the first-ever movie to be filmed entirely on IMAX cameras, the organization is once again stocking its shelves with Blu-rays. Right now, you’ll find only copies of the Greek epic in store (with a small selection of other titles available for purchase directly through the retailer’s online store), and that makes sense considering how popular it was in theaters and how awesome it will look on big TVs at home.

But this move also raises the question: Will other 4K and standard Blu-rays be coming to Best Buy stores in the near future?

A Best Buy spokesperson didn’t immediately reply to our request for comment.

When the retailer first announced it would be ditching DVD and Blu-ray sales, it cited the decades-long decline in physical media sales — which fell from its peak revenue benchmark of $16 billion in 2005 to less than $1 billion in 2024 — and planned to populate the store space with other tech-based products.

According to recent reports, the Odyssey, the nearly 3-hour-long big-screen adaptation of Homer’s epic poem, follows King Odysseus (played by Matt Damon) as he leads his soldiers on a 10-year journey home after the Trojan War. It features a stacked slate of acting talent from Anne Hathaway and Tom Holland to Zendaya, Jon Bernthal and Charlize Theron, and earned $1.8 billion at the global box office.

Those details, along with the timing of the coming holiday shopping season, have apparently inspired Best Buy to change course.

It should be noted that physical media still has value for many people. In an era when movies and TV shows (purchased or otherwise) are suddenly pulled from streamers, the allure of owning titles on DVD and Blu-ray endures. Not to mention, 4K Blu-rays offer better picture quality than their compressed, streaming versions.
