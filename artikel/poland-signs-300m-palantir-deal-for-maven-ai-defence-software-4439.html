Poland signs $300M Palantir deal for Maven AI defence software

Poland signed the contract on Friday, a five-year deal for Palantir’s Maven Smart System, which is worth an estimated $300M.

The AI software helps armed forces analyse battlefield data and make decisions. Defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz put the five-year deal’s estimated value at about $300M, Bloomberg reported.

The ministry announced the signing on Thursday, Reuters reported, citing a ministry statement. NATO and the US military already use the same system, and Palantir has mentioned it cannot switch off NATO’s Maven system.

Sources indicate that kosiniak-Kamysz called the deal historic when he spoke to reporters in Warsaw. He remarked Poland would be the sole owner of the system’s data and infrastructure.

Poland will own the “keys, servers and information” under the agreement, Kosiniak-Kamysz mentioned, according to Bloomberg.

In a recent development, “Whenever possible, we choose a Polish product,” he mentioned. Where no Polish system has comparable capabilities, the ministry picks the best one in the world, he added.

Palantir has not commented publicly on the deal or its terms.

The contract is the first binding step in a partnership that began with a cybersecurity agreement in December 2024. In October 2025, the minister and Palantir chief executive Alex Karp signed a letter of intent covering AI, IT and cybersecurity.

It has been revealed that at that signing, the minister mentioned testing had begun in December 2023. It included dozens of meetings, exercises and demonstrations across all branches of the armed forces.

It has been revealed that the deal has drawn opposition at home as protesters gathered in Warsaw earlier this month over security and data privacy. The activist group Nasze Dane also asked Poland’s human rights ombudsman to examine the cooperation.

In a recent development, the ombudsman, Sylwia Gregorczyk-Abram, wrote to the minister in October about the group’s concerns. She asked how the ministry had assessed the privacy risks. In early November, Kosiniak-Kamysz told broadcaster RMF FM he was waiting for the military to finish its analysis.

It has been revealed that poland’s Cyberspace Defence Forces Component Command led the negotiations. The minister defended the choice, noting that NATO and Ukraine’s armed forces already use Palantir’s software.

Poland is moving in the opposite direction to some of its allies. France and Germany have pledged to build a European Palantir rival, and Germany is testing 30 AI tools for its military. The Netherlands wants a full replacement for the organization’s software within two years.

In a recent development, warsaw has also been deepening defence technology ties with Washington. Earlier this week, Poland and the US signed an agreement at the Pentagon on joint defence research and development, ending 17 years of negotiations.
