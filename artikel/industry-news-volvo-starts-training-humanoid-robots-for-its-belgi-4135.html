Industry News: Volvo starts training humanoid robots for its Belgian car factory

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

An AEON humanoid robot places a part during training at Volvo Cars’ Ghent plant.

According to recent reports, volvo Cars has begun training Hexagon Robotics’ AEON humanoid robots at its plant in Ghent, Belgium, the first Volvo factory to try humanoids. A larger pilot is due in the first half of 2027, with the robots joining production from mid-2027.

Recent findings suggest that volvo Cars has started training humanoid robots at its factory in Ghent, Belgium, and plans to put them to work in production from mid-2027, the carmaker said this week. Ghent is the first Volvo plant to try humanoids.

The robots are Hexagon Robotics’ AEON, a 1.65-metre humanoid that moves on wheels rather than legs. It weighs 60kg, can carry up to 10kg and uses cameras and sensors to handle parts.

For now, staff are teaching them by demonstration to place parts into two fixtures with high precision. Later, the robots will handle more parts, move around the plant and work with its existing machines. More units will arrive for a larger pilot in the first half of 2027.

“AEON’s humanoid design means it can fit into our production as it is today, alongside our teams and existing automation,” remarked Magnus Nilsson, chief manufacturing officer at Volvo Cars.

Volvo says the aim is for the robots to take on some of the most physically demanding tasks and make work easier on staff.

Industry observers note that other European factories are running similar trials. BMW began piloting AEON at its Leipzig plant this year. In April, a humanoid passed an eight-hour shift at Siemens’ factory in Erlangen.

Recent findings suggest that some carmakers are going further. Hyundai, which owns Boston Dynamics, looks to mass-produce the Atlas humanoid from 2028.
