Industry News: Chase, Visa and Mastercard confirm vendor hacks as n0n leaks customer data

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

JPMorgan Chase indicates hackers got into data held by companies that work for the bank, and that it may include some customers’ names and email addresses. Visa and Mastercard have also confirmed incidents at outside providers.

We have learned that third parties who provide services to the firm were the target of a cyber attack.

Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses.

There is no disruption to our services.

Where appropriate, we will contact affected customers directly and offer support, including complimentary credit monitoring.

There is no connection to service disruption some customers experienced earlier this week.

— Chase (@Chase) November 19, 2026.

The bank stated the attackers accessed marketing data held by those vendors. Its own services kept running. It will offer affected customers free credit monitoring where appropriate.

Chase also remarked the attack has no link to Monday’s outage, which it blamed on a software bug in its own hardware.

Sources indicate that A ransomware gang called n0n published the data after a 48-hour deadline passed, Cybernews reported. Its researchers found about 16,000 customer email addresses, Chase account IDs and, in some cases, phone numbers. The file also holds 124 email addresses of bank staff.

All of the records seem to relate to Chase’s Reserve card, Cybernews stated. The invitation-only Visa card is for wealthy clients, usually with at least $10m in managed assets.

“This association alone makes the leak a lot more sensitive,” mentioned Aras Nazarovas, a senior security researcher at Cybernews.

Recent findings suggest that the file is only 502KB. It is not clear whether it is a sample or all the gang took. TNW has not independently verified the data.

Sources indicate that A Mastercard spokesperson told Business Insider that an outside provider exposed a limited number of email addresses. They belong to a small group of cardholders in one benefit programme. Mastercard said it has contained the exposure.

“There is no impact to the Mastercard payment network or the ability for people to use their cards,” the spokesperson said.

Visa stated its incident involved a service provider in the Asia Pacific region.

“Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted. No payments data such as card numbers were affected,” a Visa spokesperson stated.

None of the three companies has named the vendors or said how many customers the breach hit.

Sources indicate that n0n is a new gang, first tracked in mid-September, according to Cybernews. Last week it claimed to have stolen data from Expedia.

According to recent reports, the leak comes at the end of a week of outages across the US. Wall Street executives and the NSA have reportedly blamed several of them on rushed security patches for flaws that AI found, not on hackers. Earlier this week, attackers also used Optimizely’s testing tool to plant malware on major websites.
