During the trial, the prosecution described Kurtaj as a "leading light" in the teen gang Lapsus$.

A cyber criminal who hacked the maker of Grand Theft Auto has been released on licence on the same day as being sentenced to 7 years in prison.

Multiple offender Arion Kurtaj, from Oxford, was released from custody with the judge warning him: "Don't let this happen again.".

The now 21-year-old hacked several companies and individuals, including GTA 6-maker Rockstar Games, whom he tried to extort after stealing clips of the unfinished game in 2022.

Kurtaj had pleaded guilty last Monday at Woolwich Crown Court to the Rockstar hack and a handful of others which he carried out when he was 16 and 17 years old with the hacking gang Lapsus$.

Kurtaj was originally sentenced to an indefinite stay in a secure hospital due to the severity of his autism.

But, in an unusual move, he was subsequently deemed fit to stand trial and released to a normal prison in January.

Now, he has been sentenced and released on the same day as the judge considered his hospital stay and remand in prison long enough.

Recent findings suggest that he has been given a Serious Crime Prevention Order which will last five years. It restricts his activity online including barring him from using Telegram and Discord.

City of London Police noted the case "highlights the dangers that young people can end up being drawn to online".

He hacked Rockstar games, Revolut and Uber while on bail and under police protection in a Travelodge in Bicester.

He was there for protection from rival hackers who had threatened to attack him for his stolen stashes of Bitcoin.

With his computer confiscated, Kurtaj hacked Rockstar using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into his hotel room television along with a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.

He stole internal company data including 90 clips showing nearly 60 minutes of gameplay from the unfinished GTA 6, which had been shrouded in secrecy until its release last week.

He then broke into the company's internal Slack messaging system to declare: "If Rockstar does not contact me on Telegram within 24 hours I will start releasing the source code.".

GTA 6 was released to much fanfare last week.

The firm refused to co-operate so he posted the clips and source code on a forum under the username TeaPotUberHacker.

Rockstar Games said it cost the firm $5m (Â£3.8m) to recover from the hack, plus thousands of hours of staff time.

The tech giant also said the breach revealed for the first time that one of the playable characters in the game was a woman - a secret it was guarding closely for marketing purposes.

When he was sentenced he stood and smiled.

He had short hair and an unkempt beard and wore a suit and buttoned-up white shirt without a tie.

The hacker blinked frequently during the hearing.

The Judge Mr Justice Turner told him "you are a very talented young man," and warned him: "Don't let this happen again.".

Kurtaj's defence team said he found the online world a more comfortable way of interacting with other people.

"The restricted and repetitive patterns of behaviour and interests that are a core feature of ASD [autism spectrum disorder] led him to become preoccupied with the online world where he felt better able to function and exist," they noted.

They stated he was exposed to people who showed him how to cheat at games and then to "people who were involved in hacking".

During the pandemic, he was "very isolated" and his "connection to Lapsus$ provided him with a sense of community and self-worth that had been absent in his life previously," they added.

Kurtaj's Snapchat posts were the first sign for reporters that he had been released from a secure hospital.

Kurtaj and the gang's other hacks on BT/EE, Revolut and Uber used social engineering techniques - tricking people into clicking malicious links or giving up sensitive information - to gain access to internal systems.

Previously, the court heard that during his hack on Uber he posted a picture of a penis as part of his attempts to force the organization to pay a ransom.

Using the data stolen from the firms, he and other members of the gang targeted cryptocurrency investors.

The prosecution argued that the gang stole at least $300,000 (Â£230,000) from individual victims.

Kurtaj also pleaded guilty to physical assaults on workers while in custody, including multiple biting incidents.

In prison, he was caught with a mobile phone after sending a taunting text message to police and posting updates on Snapchat.
