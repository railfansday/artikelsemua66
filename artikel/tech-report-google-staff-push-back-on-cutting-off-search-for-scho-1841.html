Sources indicate that more than 540 Google employees and contractors have signed a union petition asking the company to let K-12 schools use Search without its AI features, Bloomberg reported. Google looks to turn AI off by default for students, but schools that want Search must accept the AI that comes with it.

Sources indicate that more than 540 Google employees and contractors have signed a petition asking the firm to let schools use Google Search without its AI features, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. The Alphabet Workers Union, which rallied staff over job security in July, organised the letter.

According to recent reports, “Cutting off access to Search for schools that don’t wish to use AI will not help persuade our users,” the workers wrote. They warned that schools could end up “shutting out Google products altogether.”.

The letter is addressed to CEO Sundar Pichai, head of Search Elizabeth Reid and education chief Chris Phillips. Google did not immediately comment to Bloomberg.

Earlier this month, Google said it would switch off Gemini and Search for K-12 student accounts unless schools turn them on. But AI is built into Search, so schools that switch Search back on get its AI features too. Schools that want no AI at all must go without Google Search.

The strategy drew criticism from parents and schools. Senator Ed Markey wrote to Google on 9 November, asking whether schools could keep Search with the AI switched off. In July, Common Sense Media called Google’s AI Search an “unacceptable risk” for students.

Industry observers note that the stakes are high for Google. Its Chrome operating system, which will stop getting updates in 2034, ran on 58% of laptops and tablets shipped to US schools last year, per Futuresource Consulting.

Recent findings suggest that earlier this month, a family sued Google over its daughter’s conversations with its AI tools on a school device, and Google said it was reviewing the claims. AI companies already face a growing wave of lawsuits over alleged harms to people, including children.

According to recent reports, leaving Google is not easy for schools, said Dan Carroll, co-founder of edtech startup Clever. Chromebooks are built around Google’s software, and switching search engines takes time and training.

“It’s a real strategic pickle,” Carroll stated.
