Industry News: Nvidia’s Earnings Call Is the AI Economy’s Next Stress Test

🌐 Source: Cnet • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1220 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that nvidia’s shaky future in the wake of the departure of scandal-plagued founder and CEO Jensen Huang might get clearer Tuesday as the company reports quarterly earnings. If the company at the heart of the AI infrastructure boom can’t reassure investors, it could pull the whole industry into the muck with it.

The chipmaker’s stock price has already plunged 35% from last month’s high, wiping out nearly $2 trillion in market cap and triggering Wall Street panic around a broader AI reckoning.

In the lead-up to the firm’s third-quarter earnings call, Huang issued a statement that he would return as CEO if the board of directors decides it’s in the “best interest” of shareholders and the country. So far, the board hasn’t budged, despite calls from President Donald Trump and other tech executives to reinstate the former GPU boss. The Securities and Exchange Commission is also investigating Nvidia over concerns that the company misled investors, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Sources indicate that nvidia’s finance chief and interim CEO, Colette Kress, is now tasked with convincing investors that the semiconductor giant is moving in a stable direction and appeasing fears about potential national security violations amid illegal export deals. Market confidence is also shaky amid supply chain backlogs for advanced chips and servers that remain critical to AI compute and the training of large language models.

According to recent reports, huang maneuvered Nvidia into a linchpin role for frontier AI labs and data center firms, not just by supplying hardware but also by providing equity, credit and financial backing and by mobilizing third-party capital to secure investment for the AI buildout of data centers. Nvidia’s circular financing schemes — in which the chipmaker helps fund a hyperscaler like Meta or Microsoft, which then uses the funds to buy its products — were allegedly a source of contention for Kress and other executives. Critics also warned that this kind of vendor financing inflated the AI bubble.

Recent findings suggest that according to Reuters, Kress may pull back from some of the riskiest AI financial deals, including investments in OpenAI and AI infrastructure companies such as Lambda. Already, Nvidia has significantly scaled back financing for a $250 billion OpenAI data center deal. During Tuesday’s call, Kress could lay out how the company intends to handle other outstanding deals inked by Huang, especially since Nvidia can no longer rely on a big chunk of revenue from under-the-table exports.

It has been revealed that if Nvidia keeps distancing itself from its GPU commitments, that could be a problem for the entire tech industry. Semiconductor production is an incredibly specialized field dominated by very few companies, and any changes to the supply chain could have ripple effects on global markets and vendors, including in consumer electronics and cloud services.

Nvidia supplies the lion’s share of the hardware necessary for data center construction and model training, and the firm has long mentioned that the demand for chips exceeds supply. Shortages of RAM and high-bandwidth memory slow down the production of AI servers and raise prices for cloud companies and other buyers. In an attempt to buy its way out of the bottleneck, Nvidia has committed large sums of money to secure future memory supply.

But Huang’s affair with a Chinese executive connected to the Singapore-based Megaspeed — whom US intelligence claimed was a Chinese intelligence asset — complicated an already tight supply chain. Megaspeed was under investigation for allegedly helping Chinese firms obtain high-value Nvidia chips through channels that violated US export controls.

Recent findings suggest that according to the investment research firm Culper, up to one-fifth of Nvidia’s fiscal-year 2026 compute revenues were driven by movement of some of the company’s most advanced chips into China through nonrestricted intermediary countries in a process called diversion. When advanced Nvidia chips or servers are illegally routed to Chinese buyers, those units are no longer available to legal customers in the US and allied markets.

Huang was influential in shaping the Trump administration’s chip export policies, pushing back against export controls that limited Nvidia’s sales to China. But the recent scandal prompted fresh Commerce Department restrictions on chip shipments through third countries. Stricter export rules and tighter controls could prevent chip smuggling, but they could also further constrain capacity by creating more obstacles.

In a recent development, any company that supplies essential hardware for the AI buildout is at risk if investor confidence in the industry falters. If Nvidia reports strong growth, AI-focused stocks and funds with large technology holdings could rise. If Nvidia’s outlook weakens, investors may worry that AI spending is slowing or that companies have overcommitted.

A handful of significant tech companies — colloquially known as the Magnificent Seven — make up a big share of major funds and indices. Five of them account for roughly 30% of the S&P 500 index, and AI overexposure in those businesses translates into overexposure in many individual retirement accounts and 401(k)s. Because index funds hold substantial positions in Nvidia and other large tech companies, an AI selloff could affect retirement balances even for people who don’t own Nvidia shares directly.

Industry observers note that while the biggest AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic haven’t gone public (yet), the Magnificent Seven are heavily affected by AI stock swings. Alphabet’s stock price sank as Nvidia news aftershocks reverberated through the markets, proving that top firms are not unassailable. Other tech companies heavily focused on AI infrastructure, such as Intel and CoreWeave, likewise saw their share prices decline.

In a recent development, amid growing concerns over AI dangers, Huang was one of AI’s most aggressive promoters, framing the technology as an economic and national-security imperative. In early September, the now-disgraced CEO said that artificial general intelligence, or AGI, had arrived, congratulating OpenAI on its GPT-6 Astra model, which was trained on Nvidia’s Grace Blackwell GPUs.

Huang pushed against recent warnings from AI researchers, calling for unrestrained progress on frontier models and repeatedly arguing against fresh laws and safety regulations.

Industry observers note that even before Huang’s business and personal scandals came to light, AI companies were gearing up for a post-midterm-election PR campaign, as the technology has become increasingly unpopular and the backlash against data centers has grown. Democratic Party politicians pushed a platform to shift the federal government’s lax approach toward the AI industry. Some Democrats are also poised to craft legislation to prevent an AI industry bailout in the event of a bubble pop.

Amid bipartisan calls for Huang to testify before Congress, many politicians are also seeking to investigate high-ranking members of US intelligence and the Commerce Department for allowing export loopholes or for failing to act on knowledge of Nvidia’s China-related deals.

Kress might be able to lead Nvidia with tact and diligence, separating the company from some of its riskiest deals. But the national security implications of her predecessor’s relationships have opened up a can of worms that no CEO can close on their own.

The federal government is ruled by inertia, but right now there’s some investigative momentum aimed directly at the major AI players. That will almost certainly ice out some investor hype. Whatever comes out of Nvidia’s earnings call, it’s clear the AI industry is facing a fresh reckoning that didn’t exist just months ago.
