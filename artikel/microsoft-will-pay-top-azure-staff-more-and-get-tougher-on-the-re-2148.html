Microsoft’s office in Dubai Internet City, Dubai, 3 November 2026.

Microsoft is making pay in its Azure cloud division more performance-based, with extra stock awards for top performers and formal meetings with managers for those with lower results, Bloomberg reported, citing a memo from Azure chief Arun Ulagaratchagan.

Microsoft will tie pay in its Azure cloud unit more closely to how well staff do, Bloomberg reported on Friday. Top performers will get extra stock. Staff with weaker results will face formal meetings with their bosses.

It has been revealed that the plan was set out in a memo from Arun Ulagaratchagan, who runs Azure. Bloomberg has seen the memo.

“Pay-for-performance only works if we are equally rigorous on both sides: recognizing exceptional impact and being honest when performance falls short,” Ulagaratchagan wrote.

He told staff that meeting today’s bar means closing past gaps and showing “a meaningful step-change in impact.” AI is reshaping how the world works, he stated, and the competition has never been tougher.

A Microsoft spokesperson confirmed the change, saying it brings “meaningful additional awards” for the best performers. Staff will also get clearer goals and more direct feedback from their bosses, the spokesperson stated.

Recent findings suggest that azure sales grew 48% last quarter, but the unit is also under cost pressure. In October, an Azure executive reportedly asked teams to find 20% cost cuts, partly to fund higher pay. Microsoft disputed that report.

Recent findings suggest that last month, Microsoft offered buyouts to about 60,000 employees, around a quarter of its staff. A pay spreadsheet built by staff indicated the largest stock awards already going to Cloud and AI workers.

Other tech giants are pushing staff harder too. Google has offered voluntary buyouts in more countries. Its workers’ union wants it to stop rating staff on a curve.
