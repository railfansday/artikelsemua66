Uber may be breaking up with Waymo, but it’s also gaining a new robotaxi BFF: Amazon. Starting today, Uber riders in Las Vegas can be matched with Amazon-owned Zoox’s purpose-built, bidirectional robotaxis.

It’s a first for both companies: the first time Uber is adding a purpose-built, steering-wheel-free autonomous vehicle to its network, and the first time that Zoox is opening up its fleet to a third-party app. Uber says that anyone ordering an UberX, Uber Comfort, Uber Electric, Uber Comfort Electric, or Wait & Save in Sin City can get matched with a Zoox at no extra charge. Just like with Uber’s other AV partnerships (like Waymo in Phoenix and Austin, for now), riders can toggle a preference in the app’s Ride Preferences settings to boost their chances of getting an autonomous pod.

In a recent development, unlike Uber’s human drivers, Zoox has some geographic limitations. The organization’s robotaxis only cover heavy tourist zones, like the Strip, downtown Las Vegas, and the Arts District. And Zoox isn’t abandoning its standalone app; it will continue to operate independently alongside the Uber integration.

Alongside the launch, Uber and Zoox unveiled a multiyear deal making them the official rideshare and robotaxi partners for MGM Resorts International in Las Vegas. Anyone who has ever tried to catch a ride at the Bellagio or MGM Grand knows that resort pickup zones are notoriously chaotic. For autonomous vehicles, navigating sprawling, poorly mapped casino entrances — where valets, idling tour buses, and confused tourists all mill about — is a major technical headache.

Sources indicate that to keep Zoox’s pods from getting stuck in endless resort traffic loops, the partnership goes beyond simple branding. MGM, Uber, and Zoox say they are rolling out physical infrastructure tweaks, like clearer on-property wayfinding and custom in-app guidance designed to make pickups and drop-offs actually work smoothly for driverless cars.

Recent findings suggest that uber and Zoox aren’t stopping at Vegas. The two companies already have their eyes their next major market: Los Angeles. Zoox plans to launch initial standalone operations in LA through its own app before the end of 2026, followed by a full Uber integration sometime in 2027.

Uber’s playbook of welcoming a variety of small and large robotaxi players into its app came after the firm pivoted away from building its own self-driving tech. Now it’s attempting to position itself as an indispensable platform for every autonomous vehicle operator, including Motional, WeRide, Avride, and Zoox.

Recent findings suggest that of course, Uber’s strategy has had some drawbacks, too. Waymo co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana recently announced an end to her firm’s exclusive partnership with Uber in Austin and Atlanta by January 2028, calling it a “crapshoot.”.
