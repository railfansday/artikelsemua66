Tech Report: Microsoft says services fully restored after day-long global outage

Products such as Microsoft Teams and Outlook have been out of service for much of Friday.

Microsoft has said its services have been fully restored after a worldwide outage affected several of its tools, causing disruption.

The tech firm stated early on Saturday morning that efforts to address the underlying problem had been completed and customers using Outlook, Teams and Copilot should no longer be affected.

Recent findings suggest that the outage hit more than 70 countries, disrupting everything from business operations to government services and personal users. More than 430 million people use the affected products worldwide.

Microsoft apologised for the technical issue, which it remarked was introduced during a "network capacity upgrade" and was not caused by a cyber-attack or an artificial intelligence agent.

Experts noted the event was a "stark reminder" of how heavily businesses and vital services rely on a small group of significant tech providers.

Owners began reporting they were having trouble accessing Microsoft 365 products on Thursday.

By Friday, the far-reaching matter was affecting institutions from the Japanese government and the Philippines Supreme Court to the Australian spy agency, and trains, hospitals and courts in North America.

Services were beginning to recover by Friday afternoon UK time, nearly 24 hours after the challenges first began to emerge.

In the UK, some parts of the NHS were affected, as were some schools and police forces.

UK cyber minister Liz Lloyd mentioned the incident had "limited" impact on critical frontline services such as 999 calls and that there had been no disruption to the transport network.

In Canada, however, the national rail network said passengers may not be able to receive email updates about their trips, and hospitals across the country also had challenges with emails.

In the US, staff members from the House of Representatives told Politico it had set back an entire day of legislative work as everything was on Microsoft's Outlook.

Singapore's government acknowledged some of its systems had been impacted, and in India, the outage disrupted work for many on Friday.

In Turkey, advisers and officials attending the COP31 climate summit in Antalya were left frustrated by being unable to access Outlook.

The problems also affected smaller businesses and individuals.

Pete notes his business has been brought to a standstill.

Peter Pentz, a winemaker in South Africa, said his entire business had been effectively brought to a "standstill".

His winery's communication with exports was also brought to a standstill, he said, and "critical production work" had been halted as the Cape West Coast winery "relies on these systems for parts of our day-to-day operations".

Georgia from Staffordshire in England said she had been due to complete her first house purchase after waiting eight months to get to this point - but the outage has delayed that.

"The solicitor mentioned there may be a delay due to not being able to contact other agencies," she noted.

"We're not the only one completing today - there are hundreds of houses and for them to ring everyone in time is unfeasible.".

Microsoft first acknowledged users in the Americas were unable to access Exchange Online mailboxes at 17:54 GMT on Thursday, and shortly after warned the disruption was possibly affecting Microsoft 365 globally.

It launched an investigation and engineers began working to restore services, but by 04:36 on Friday Microsoft was reporting only a "partial recovery" in some parts of the world.

By 13:32, the tech giant said there had been "broader improvements" in North America, "sustained improvements" across much of Europe and "encouraging recovery signals" in the Asia Pacific region.

Microsoft noted it restored services gradually due to the scale of the problem and to help ensure services remained stable once they were up and running again.

"We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners," a Microsoft spokesperson noted.

"We know the trust they place in our services and take that responsibility extremely seriously.".

A full post-incident report will be released once Microsoft has completed a review.

Experts remarked that while workers may have been happy to not have email access for a working day, the incident demonstrated the dependence many organisations have on these cloud-based services.

"The global Microsoft outage is a stark reminder of how heavily we rely on a handful of big tech providers," stated Rob Demain, chief executive of cybersecurity firm e2e-assure.

Matt Saunders, field chief technology officer at tech consultancy Adaptavist, remarked it would be challenging to mitigate against a similar problem.

"Classic IT disaster planning strategies suggest using a second service for resilience, but in practice, it's almost impossible for a business to function without tools such as Microsoft," he said.

"In the wake of this outage, organisations must have a plan in place for how to work without such tools, for when incidents like this occur.".
