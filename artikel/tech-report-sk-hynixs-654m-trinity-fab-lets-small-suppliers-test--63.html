Tech Report: SK Hynix’s $654m Trinity Fab lets small suppliers test on a real line

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 16 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (519 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, SK Hynix mentioned on Monday it will spend 1.4tn won ($1.05bn) over five years on South Korea’s smaller chip suppliers. The plan includes Trinity Fab, an 874.6bn won ($654m) test site. There, suppliers can try their materials and machines on 12-inch wafers under factory conditions.

Sources indicate that the support covers four stages, from research and testing to mass production and sales, Reuters reported. SK Hynix will pay up to half of a supplier’s early development costs in advance. It will also pay for the work if a project misses its targets.

Trinity Fab sits inside Y1, the first fab at SK Hynix’s Yongin cluster south of Seoul. SK Hynix plans to open it in May 2027. Its 3,305-square-metre cleanroom will hold about 50 tools, from lithography to etching and measurement, according to Bloomberg.

Three backers share the bill. SK Hynix puts in 443.2bn won and the central government 373.6bn won, with 57.7bn won from local governments, Reuters reported. An independent non-profit foundation will run the site, so suppliers can test without exposing their intellectual property.

SK Hynix calls it the world’s first open-access testbed for mass-production readiness. Suppliers can rent about a fifth of the space and install their own equipment there, MoneyToday reported after a site visit.

In a recent development, smaller Korean firms struggle to prove their products in a real factory. Cleanrooms are costly, and chipmaking links hundreds of processes that no small supplier can copy on its own.

Yang Ji-dong, who leads SK Hynix’s Yongin planning team, compared it to a race.

“In a marathon, the final 10-kilometer stretch is called the ‘wall of the marathon’ because it is the most difficult,” he told MoneyToday.

Sources indicate that expectations for the time saved differ. Equipment development and certification usually takes about five years. Lee Jae-ho, vice chairman of equipment maker TES, told MoneyToday that Trinity Fab could cut about one year. Seeking Alpha cited a bigger goal: 30% to 50% off validation times of two to three years.

The plan adds to a 2tn won financing programme for suppliers. SK Hynix launched it in October with four banks and the Korea Trade Insurance Corporation. SK Hynix is also doubling its interest-free payment support fund to 100bn won.

Sources indicate that korea’s two memory makers are riding an AI boom. SK Hynix posted record quarterly profit in October, and Samsung did too. Seoul has also put chips at the centre of its 2027 budget.

Rivals are closing in. China’s CXMT aims to make AI memory despite low yields. SK Hynix plans to invest 600tn won in four fabs at Yongin, and its suppliers will have to keep pace.

Lee Hyun-cheol, SK Hynix’s head of purchasing strategy, framed it as a national effort in remarks to MoneyToday.

“As global semiconductor hegemony competition intensifies, a ‘one team’ system where the upstream ecosystem and large corporations move as one is essential,” he said.
