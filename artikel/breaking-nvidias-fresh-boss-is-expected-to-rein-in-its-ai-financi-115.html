Breaking: Nvidia’s fresh boss is expected to rein in its AI financing, WSJ reports

Recent findings suggest that nvidia’s interim chief executive, Colette Kress, is expected to rein in its financing of the AI industry, The Wall Street Journal reported on Sunday. Jensen Huang had already started that pullback in his final months as CEO, the Journal’s Robbie Whelan, Anissa Gardizy and Kate Clark wrote. He told neocloud partners to get their customers to pay upfront.

In a recent development, kress, Nvidia’s finance chief since 2013, was already wary of some of the deals Huang made, according to the Journal. Startups that counted on Nvidia’s support now expect an even bigger pullback in Nvidia financing.

In a recent development, nvidia confirmed its financing programme in July. About a month later, Huang and Kress started questioning the return on some of the deals, the Journal reported.

In August, Nvidia cut the scale of its commitment to OpenAI’s data centre lease in Piketon, Ohio. OpenAI signed the lease with SoftBank’s SB Energy. Nvidia scaled back after Kress became sceptical of the deal, according to the Journal.

Huang also told his finance team that massive cloud providers planned to use other chips. That left Nvidia with more chip wafers and memory than it needed, which it had already committed to buy. He asked staff to find a way to judge whether the guarantees Nvidia gave smaller companies were worth it. By the end of August, Nvidia had paused some deals that were in progress, the Journal reported.

In October, neocloud chief executives met at Nvidia’s GTC conference in Berlin. Huang told them to secure payment upfront from their customers rather than simply rent out his chips. That is according to people in attendance who spoke to the Journal. Two executives who heard his remarks, and one briefed on them, gave his words as:.

Industry observers note that “You’re not charging what you should be charging. I don’t even need you guys. I could just give all this capacity to Elon! Elon wants it.”.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia took part in a record 129 venture deals over the past year, the Journal reported. It did 49 in 2023. That was 27% of global venture deal value. Nvidia’s latest quarterly filing lists $36bn of off-balance-sheet AI cloud agreements, the Journal mentioned. Those are its neocloud backstops.

Some founders of companies with little or no revenue told the Journal they could struggle to raise money without Nvidia. UBS analyst Tim Arcuri estimated that a pullback could take $50bn or more out of the market.

According to recent reports, several deals are still open. Nscale is negotiating with Nvidia and other investors to back a $45bn data centre for Anthropic in West Virginia. Nvidia had been in talks to guarantee its debt. Nvidia offered to license Reflection AI’s technology and hire some of its staff. That deal was not done before Huang left. Nvidia is also finalising an investment in Thinking Machines Lab at a $40bn valuation, according to the Journal.

Huang defended Nvidia’s investments in an interview with Fortune in London in October. He stated Nvidia only takes minority stakes, so other investors must back the companies too.

“We’ve created an industry that the VCs alone cannot fund,” Huang told Fortune.

It has been revealed that marc Boroditsky is chief revenue officer at Nebius, a neocloud Nvidia has invested in. He told Fortune:.

“If it’s all going to come down to Nvidia’s capital, we would have run out already,” Boroditsky remarked.

According to recent reports, huang resigned as CEO on Thursday, and the board named Kress interim chief executive. She keeps her finance role. An Nvidia spokesperson told CNBC:.

Industry observers note that “We are in the process of selecting a search firm for the NVIDIA CEO role. We have not formally engaged any potential candidates.”.

Huang has discussed a return with allies, the Financial Times reported. On Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News that the board should look outside the manufacturer. He also wants answers from the board within 10 days on its export compliance, Axios reported.

Industry observers note that nvidia reports its third-quarter results on Tuesday, 17 November. The earnings call will be Kress’s first public appearance in charge of the tech giant, The Information noted.
