Breaking: FBI investigates a breach of its NCIC crime database as USDA is also hit

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The J. Edgar Hoover Building, the FBI’s headquarters in Washington, DC.

According to recent reports, the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center (NCIC), CNN reported on Friday. Police across the US use the database to check warrants and run background checks. Evan Perez cited two people briefed on the investigation. It is the second intrusion at the bureau in recent months.

It has been revealed that NBC News confirmed the investigation later that evening through two people briefed on the matter. The FBI had no comment as of Friday night, NBC reported. It has not said who may be behind the breach.

Sources indicate that NCIC links federal, state and local agencies so that law enforcement information is available nationwide. A former FBI official told CNN that the accuracy of the records is also at stake. The official once led investigations into cybercriminal groups.

“My concern isn’t simply whether hackers gained access to sensitive information, although that is an matter, but whether they could compromise the accuracy of the information that officers rely on,” the former official told CNN.

Bureau officials had already learned of the incident on Thursday when FBI Director Kash Patel went on Fox News to promote its cybersecurity work, CNN reported. He spoke about Operation Winter Shield as if it were still under way. The effort urged companies to revision their systems. CNN reported that the operation ended months ago.

Industry observers note that “Other administrations could have made these updates, but they didn’t. We are staying ahead of the curve and making up for lost time,” Patel noted.

According to recent reports, in September, the cybercrime group ShinyHunters claimed it had stolen personal data on FBI staff through a bureau jobs website. The FBI opened an investigation. The group noted it took data on all bureau staff, about 38,000 people, the BBC reported. Authorities have since arrested and charged a Canadian cybersecurity researcher and author over that breach, according to CNN.

The FBI news lands in the same week as a cyberattack on the Department of Agriculture. The USDA disclosed a cybersecurity incident internally this week and has taken numerous applications offline over several days, Nextgov/FCW and Government Executive reported on Thursday. They cited three people familiar with the matter and an internal email. The outages hit many of the financial tools the agency uses to pay farmers.

Sources indicate that the department told staff on Thursday that hackers had targeted one of its systems, NBC News reported. USDA officials with knowledge of the matter told NBC the breach hit the farm payment system. As of Friday, it had not reached other systems, they noted. They warned that the hackers could find a way into more sensitive information. The USDA has not commented publicly, the BBC reported.

In a recent development, both breaches follow a run of outages at banks, air traffic control and the National Security Agency. The Trump administration states those outages are not connected and that cyberattacks did not cause them. Banks and the NSA have reportedly blamed patches for AI-found flaws.

Recent findings suggest that an NSA classified network that went down during maintenance on Tuesday is working again. Sources told NBC there was no sign of a cyberattack. The White House also changed its top cyber official this week, with Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies replacing Sean Cairncross. Neither the FBI nor the USDA has stated who is responsible or what data the hackers took.
