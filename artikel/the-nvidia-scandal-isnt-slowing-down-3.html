Jensen Huang’s absence has left the AI industry in limbo.﻿.

In a recent development, for years, Jensen Huang has had an almost unprecedented power to shape AI’s advancement as Nvidia’s CEO, cofounder, and president. As The Verge wrote last week, Nvidia’s chips are “the bedrock upon which advanced AI technology in the US is being built.” Ever-increasing demand has consistently outpaced supply, leading AI CEOs to essentially bow at Huang’s feet as they vie for more compute ahead of their much-hyped IPOs. Nvidia also has a varied investment portfolio of up-and-coming AI companies from neoclouds to infrastructure firms, worth nearly $100 billion, and Huang has President Donald Trump’s ear. He’s even been called Trump’s “most influential adviser” when it comes to technology. As of last Wednesday, however, Huang — a pivotal figure in the industry — is no longer CEO of Nvidia.

Sources indicate that since Huang’s resignation last week, the scandal over his multiple affairs has shown no sign of waning. After the announcement, Nvidia shares dropped 14 percent, making it one of the worst weeks on the market for the firm in the past year and a half. And the revelations kept coming. Hours after the resignation, Reuters reported that Huang had had a third affair with a second employee (though the employee was unnamed). We learned that Huang had reportedly misled Nvidia leaders about his infidelity, that Nvidia was allegedly linked to the supply of nearly $12 billion of its chips to China, and that alleged Chinese intelligence asset Alice Huang’s companies reportedly promised advanced chips to Chinese AI company DeepSeek.

According to recent reports, there’s reportedly been a petition to bring back Huang by some Nvidia employees, as well as reports of Huang assembling a “war room” of allies to float a comeback as Nvidia CEO. Lawmakers have begun investigations into the alleged chip-smuggling and other offenses. Every day, there’s increasing evidence of fallout inside the Trump administration. And as Nvidia’s quarterly earnings on Tuesday loom, the future of the manufacturer and the AI industry as a whole remain in flux.

According to recent reports, what does an Nvidia without Huang look like? It’s hard to say, since he’s been at the helm for 33 years. CFO Colette Kress has taken on the role of interim CEO, and the broader AI world is waiting to see where one of the world’s most valuable companies — and one that helped shape the industry’s every milestone — goes from here.

Sources indicate that nvidia is such a large manufacturer that the implications of Huang’s departure “will take years to play out,” remarked Stephen Straus, CEO of KUNGFU.AI, an AI consulting firm. “[Nvidia has] their market share … they have the world’s best chips, they have the most manufacturing capacity … and they have a whole ecosystem built around what they’ve done. None of that changes with one person.” While there’s been discussion of Huang’s departure speeding along an AI bubble burst, Straus compares the situation to the dot-com bust — which didn’t stop the growth of the internet itself.

It has been revealed that huang’s resignation may not cause a domino effect in AI, but longer-term AI funding initiatives championed by him may eventually be affected, says Lior Prosor, managing partner at Deep33, a tech VC firm. Huang championed fundraising for the broader AI buildout: In August, Nvidia signed deals with six large financial institutions for about $500 billion to fund AI infrastructure expansion, and those agreements or future ones could look different if “the most prominent CEO in the AI landscape is no longer orchestrating such deals,” wrote Morningstar senior equity analyst Brian Colello in a note last week on Monday.

Some lawmakers were already planning to pull CEOs before Congress.

Far downstream of the biggest companies, smaller AI startups are wondering how the effects will trickle down. Michal Miernowski, co-founder and CEO of Amperos Health, which builds AI agents for healthcare billing, sits in the applied AI space about two layers removed from Nvidia. He doesn’t anticipate Amperos being directly affected much by the moves, since it relies on a mix of frontier AI models and open-source ones, but a cooling of the larger AI market — whether due to the Nvidia scandal or broader concerns — could stall business as well. There’s also more to come on Nvidia’s side, from the organization’s investigation into Huang’s actions to Congressional inquiries. Evan Gotlob, partner at DarrowEverett LLP and a former state and federal prosecutor, mentioned he thinks “there’s definitely more” to the story about potential leaks to China and suspicious chip dealing. He added the board’s investigation would “obviously” continue despite his departure: “The investigation is really not going to turn out anything good for him.”.

Industry observers note that some new lawmakers already planned to haul CEOs to Capitol Hill to testify under oath. The AI populism movement is growing, as well as the blowback against data centers, which was a key talking point in Democrats’ campaigns during the midterms. The revelations around Huang, a major Trump ally, provide an early opportunity to take action.

“We want to see a Democratic Congress investigate Nvidia for anti-competitive practices that pass off costs to consumers, what the manufacturer knew about chip smuggling operations with China, and what Nvidia received in exchange for donations to the Trump campaign and ballroom,” Sacha Haworth, founder and CEO of The Tech Oversight Project, told The Verge.

According to recent reports, AI safety researchers also worry that in the worst-case scenario, the instability around last week’s events could speed up potentially dangerous AI milestones like recursive self-improvement, i.e., powerful AI that can autonomously improve and build future versions of itself. Marius Hobbhahn, CEO and founder of Apollo Research, an AI safety firm and third-party auditor, noted he worries that the volatility stemming from Huang’s departure “actually increases loss of control risk because companies feel even more revenue pressure and therefore are more willing to cut corners on safety.”.

In a recent development, early last week, before Huang’s resignation, the general consensus among Silicon Valley heavyweights, analysts, and politicians was that Huang’s “irreplaceable” impact would mean a return to the status quo for Nvidia. Morningstar senior equity analyst Brian Colello wrote in a note that day that the firm “can’t rule out … Nvidia returning to business as usual” since Huang is the “only CEO the organization has ever known.” Now, as Huang reportedly assembles allies to push for a comeback the likes of which haven’t been seen since the 2023 Sam Altman coup, the entire AI industry is waiting to see which way it will go.
