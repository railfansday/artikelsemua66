Tech Report: Google’s new AI tool just proved Signal encryption really works – here’s how

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Researchers at Google DeepMind used AI to formally verify portions of Signal‘s underlying code — meaning they’ve mathematically proven that at least parts of the encrypted messaging app’s code do what they say, without errors. That’s reassuring given how many vulnerabilities AI systems have discovered in software lately, and how many people’s communications are protected by Signal’s cryptography. WhatsApp and Google Messages use the same encryption.

Also: How AI security failures are endangering your supply chain.

It has been revealed that deepMind used AlphaProof Code, “a novel agentic framework for formal verification at unprecedented depth that combines human expertise with AI agents,” the researchers remarked. They added that formal verification “is the highest standard of security assurance,” but, without automation at scale like AI systems, difficult and time-consuming.

AI companies like OpenAI have been on a math-breakthrough tear as of late, in some cases solving nearly century-old math problems with AI. By computing at such vast scale, AI models can rapidly accelerate much of the manual work mathematicians do.

Using AlphaProof Code and building on earlier verification work by the Signal Foundation, DeepMind researchers divided the verification task into two camps. Humans worked with agents to identify what they needed to prove, and multiple agents running Gemini Carbon, the firm’s latest model, then executed it. The agents also audited the proof and “highlighted areas that needed a second look by a human — just 1% of the codebase, a massive time savings,” the researchers wrote.

For good measure, researchers tested AlphaProof Code’s ability to distinguish correct from flawed code by showing it two previously fixed Signal bugs, for which it produced correct proofs.

Recent findings suggest that “Our work marks the first time AI has been used to formally verify a widely deployed cryptographic protocol at this depth,” the researchers wrote. “It’s also the first time the implementation of a widely deployed protocol like this has been formally verified by any method, with or without AI.”.

Normally, researchers need to manually develop a proof to verify software, which takes much longer. That labor-intensive barrier has made formal verification a distant promised land, until now. The DeepMind team’s verification took a month to do what might normally take experts years.

DeepMind did not verify all of Signal’s code, but noted that this test is a step toward improved security infrastructure at a time when AI is disrupting the entire cybersecurity space.

“Having the core Signal protocol rigorously verified with automated theorem proving gives us even greater confidence that our code delivers on its privacy guarantees,” the researchers wrote.

It has been revealed that the breakthrough reiterates how deeply AI is both a massive risk for cybersecurity and an equally powerful fix. The year 2026 has been especially marked by AI-related security incidents, many of which involve agents. At the same time, companies like Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI are developing models geared specifically toward cybersecurity, including Anthropic’s Claude Mythos.

Recent findings suggest that deepMind did not share any aims to release its findings as a consumer product, though the researchers are open-sourcing their code to help others with formal verification efforts. “Verifying Signal’s core cryptography lays the groundwork to secure other vital systems, from medical device firmware to power grid software,” DeepMind added.
