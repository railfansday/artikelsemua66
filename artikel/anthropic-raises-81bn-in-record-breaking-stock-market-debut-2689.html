Watch: How did Anthropic's stock market debut unfold?

Anthropic, the company behind AI chatbot Claude, has raised a record $81bn (Â£62bn) in a stock market debut that will make it one of the most valuable companies in the world.

A surge of investor appetite to buy shares in the loss-making business meant more stock was sold than originally planned. Anthropic's shares have been priced at $83, up from the proposed $70-$78 range.

Shares will begin trading later on Thursday in Upcoming York, valuing the five year-old firm at around $1.5 trillion.

It has been revealed that the flotation is being seen as a crucial test for fast-growing AI sector. Anthropic is the first purely AI-focused firm to publicly sell its shares.

Vast amounts of money have been poured into AI infrastructure and the development of AI based tools. But questions are being raised over whether and when those investments will pay off.

The seven founders, who set up Anthropic up in 2021, will become multi-billionaires immediately.

The company's $1.5tn estimated valuation is on a "diluted" basis, a broader reflection of the firm's value that takes things like employee stock options into account. On a basic share count Anthropic is worth about $1.1tn, more valuable than Walmart and Samsung but less than Tesla and Meta.

But strong demand could push its share price, and overall valuation, higher, once shares are publicly traded.

Anthropic's revenues have ballooned in the last year, to $33bn in the nine months to September this year, as the market for its tools, designed to speed up processes including coding, writing and digesting information, has grown.

But net losses have also increased, reaching $104bn for the same period, according to the firm's IPO document.

Leah Bramwell from Canaccord Wealth noted there had been strong interest in Anthropic shares "across the board" including both institutional and retail - that is private individual - investors, which she said suggested it was not simply a "retail-driven frenzy into into AI names".

In total Anthropic could raise $107bn. Over $81bn comes from the public share sale, with an additional $26bn from separate investments from a group of tech firms including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia.

Anthropic could raise a further $12bn through an additional provision, which is normal practice, to permit further shares to be sold if demand is very high.

Elon Musk's SpaceX raised an initial $75bn in its IPO in June this year.

The company soared in value when it joined the Nasdaq, reaching $2.2tn, although in the weeks that followed, the share price fell.

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei co-founded firm five years ago.

OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT and Anthropic's biggest AI rival, is expected to go public next year. Until then, Anthropic will be the only public organization in the US that is entirely focused on the technology.

While there is excitement around Anthropic's listing, there is also some concern from investors about its future ability to generate profits in a competitive area.

Yang Wang, a principle analyst at Counterpoint Research, stated the central issue was whether it could "keep pace" as hundreds of billions of dollars in financial obligations come due in the next couple of years.

However, the share sale would give investors "direct exposure to a frontier-model developer with the potential to shape the next stage of productivity", Wang said.

Anthropic was started by seven former OpenAI employees, led by brother and sister Dario and Daniela Amodei.

Dario Amodei has been outspoken about the risks posed by AI, amounting in his view to a possible threat to human existence.

The firm has called for a slowdown in the development of AI, and a greater focus on safeguards while forging ahead with looks to grow swiftly and boost its financing through the public markets.

OpenAI has voiced similar sentiments and delayed its prepares to list on the stock market in order to address AI safety concerns.

Technology stocks have lost value in recent weeks following the US midterm elections. Democrats, who now control of both chambers of Congress, are expected to be tougher on AI regulation. Some Democrat candidates opposed the rapid build of data centres required to run AI.

But Anthropic went ahead with its market listing, despite the market volatility, because it needed to tap into additional funds, said Marta Norton, who heads strategy at investment firm Empower.

"AI is financially demanding," Norton told the BBC. "The public markets open up the financial spigot a little bit more and so the time has come.".

Broader anxieties about the AI industry remain.

Competition from low-cost rival models is growing.

Expensive computer chips, necessary for the industry to operate, are in short supply.

And the US Federal Reserve is concerned about high AI-related debt being financed through "complex and opaque" structures, according to minutes from its October meeting to discuss interest rates, as well as the high concentration of AI exposure among a small number of firms.
