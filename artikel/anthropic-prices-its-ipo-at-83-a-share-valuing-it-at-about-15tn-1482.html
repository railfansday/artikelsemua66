Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, valuing it at about $1.5tn

Anthropic has priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range it marketed, people familiar with the matter told Natasha Mascarenhas and Bailey Lipschultz of Bloomberg, who reported the price first. The manufacturer also sold more shares than it planned. Its stock starts trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT.

Recent findings suggest that at $83, Anthropic is worth about $1.49tn on a fully diluted basis, the Financial Times reported. Other outlets have since confirmed the price with their own sources. Anthropic has not announced it.

The company had been weighing a price well above $80, and orders had reached close to seven times the shares on offer by Sunday.

Anthropic had marketed 801.4 million shares, and its seven co-founders offered another 23.6 million, Bloomberg reported. With the higher price, the Anthropic IPO should raise at least $68bn. A $26bn private placement with strategic partners, at the same price, takes the total past $94bn.

That beats SpaceX, which raised $75bn in its June IPO, or $86.2bn once its bankers sold extra shares. Anthropic’s bankers have the same option, the FT reported, so the total could still rise.

By value, it would be the second-biggest IPO ever, behind SpaceX’s $1.77tn debut, Fortune reported.

Buyers should try “not to think of your purchase in purely transactional terms”, Anthropic wrote in its prospectus, the FT reported.

The firm said profits would be balanced against “the public good”.

Sources indicate that investors expected a $2tn valuation over the summer, the FT reported. The IPO lands as AI stocks slide. The Nasdaq 100 has lost 13% since its early October peak, according to the FT. Nvidia fell for a fifth day on Wednesday, Bloomberg reported, a week after Jensen Huang’s exit as chief executive.

According to recent reports, the offering will leave Anthropic with more than $200bn on its balance sheet, the FT reported. The company plans to spend that on compute and infrastructure. According to its investors, it would rather not sell shares again, and it wants an investment-grade credit rating so it can borrow instead.

It already carries $6.2bn of debt, which it calls “substantial” in its filings, the Associated Press reported. Its long-term benefit trust will be able to elect or remove a majority of the board.

The listing is also a payday for Anthropic’s venture backers, several of which planned to buy more shares in the offering.
