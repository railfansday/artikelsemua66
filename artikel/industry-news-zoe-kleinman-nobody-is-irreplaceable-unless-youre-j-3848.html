Remember the tech boss and his head of HR, both married to other people, who were caught on camera at a Coldplay concert with their arms wrapped around each other?

Their horrified reaction went viral, and the pair both resigned. Astronomer, the firm where they both worked, mentioned their actions had fallen short of its standards.

Compared with the scandal which has engulfed Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang in recent weeks, that Coldplay kiss cam moment now feels rather tame.

Huang admitted to having had an affair, reportedly with a woman accused of being a Chinese spy.

There were suggestions that she may have been involved in smuggling Nvidia chips into China, in breach of a US export ban.

Concerns about national security had reached the desk of US President Donald Trump.

There were other stories about some of Huang's relationships with female colleagues.

For most of us, that would without question amount to instant, and irreversible, dismissal.

But while Huang briefly left the firm, he was just as swiftly reinstated, and welcomed back with open arms.

As I watched footage of him being greeted with cheers, applause, high fives and a big hug back at the firm's HQ on Monday, I wondered how many disgraced bosses would kill to be in his shoes.

"Imagine being that Coldplay CEO reading all the comments on here about how it's OK if you're actually a good enough CEO," quipped menswear writer Derek Guy on X, external.

How Nvidia is reacting to Jensen Huang's return as CEO.

Huang has blown out of the water the old adage that nobody is irreplaceable.

Despite names of potential permanent replacements being bandied around, Nvidia's board evidently reached the same conclusion - interim chief executive Colette Kress was in for less than two weeks.

So how on Earth did he manage it?

Well firstly, Huang is the boss of a organization that has manoeuvred itself into a uniquely powerful position.

Nvidia's computer chips are considered the best in the business for driving AI.

Demand for its latest tech is through the roof and the firm sets its prices accordingly: Its Rubin and Vera GPUs sell for tens of thousands of dollars each, and one data centre might contain hundreds of them.

So, the US AI sector relies on Nvidia, Trump is determined that the American AI industry needs to be the best in the world, and Huang is the only leader the company has ever known.

He's been at the helm for 33 years and he's a computer scientist himself, renowned for being very hands-on with the development process. (Whether the boss's input is always wanted is another matter, but it's always there.).

Long hours are normal and negative feedback is often given in front of everyone else.

When he wants something to happen, he doesn't stop until it does: one of his early ideas was to halve the chip production cycle, in order to get one step ahead of the competition with their longer lead times for orders.

In a recent development, lots of experts told him it couldn't be done. Until eventually, they did it.

Not only does Huang have technical expertise, he's also a charismatic figurehead, representing Nvidia at events around the world in his trademark leather jacket, rubbing shoulders with world leaders, royalty, and fellow tech bosses.

For many, he's become something of a cult figure. Nvidia sometimes sells limited edition clothing at conference pop up stalls. A jumper featuring a cartoon image of Huang costs $178 (Â£137).

Nvidia's clothing lines quickly sell out, bought by fans of the tech giant.

In addition, Huang has powerful allies, and they are mostly powerful men. US President Donald Trump personally pushed for his return to Nvidia, while Elon Musk noted it would be "an honour" to work with him at SpaceX.

He is one of very few people who knows both Trump and China's President Xi Jinping personally - and somehow he has surfed the geopolitical tension between the two continents.

He has said he thinks both China and the US can win the AI race and called on them to pull together.

And finally there's the bottom line, which is always money.

Nvidia value climbs by almost $1tn and tech stocks rally after Jensen Huang returns.

Recent findings suggest that huang's brief departure from Nvidia caused economic turmoil. The firm's value sank by a whopping $1tn before it climbed back up again.

It affected not only pension funds and investors around the world, but also Nvidia's own staff.

Tech employees often get to own valuable stocks in the firm they work for as part of their pay packages.

According to recent reports, nvidia employees have been sitting on a goldmine. It wasn't really in their interests to see that drain away because of the boss's love life.

With a firm grip on the company he co-founded, cult-like status in tech circles and friends in high places, Jensen Huang created a situation where even national security concerns over the US's biggest rival couldn't topple him.
