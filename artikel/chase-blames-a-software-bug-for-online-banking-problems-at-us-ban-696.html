Chase blames a software bug for online banking problems at US banks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that JPMorgan Chase blamed a software bug in its own on-premise hardware for Monday’s online banking problems at several big US banks. The bank said in a post on X that there was no evidence of a cyberattack. It aims to fix the concern by the end of the day.

JPMorganChase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems – there is no evidence this is cyber-related. It is taking longer than usual to service some customers.

Where we were party to a transaction involving other banks, those banks may have been impacted, but this is not a systemic issue.

Customers still have access to their accounts and funds.

We’re working diligently to resolve this by the end of the day today.

— Chase (@Chase) November 16, 2026.

Other banks felt it only where they were party to a transaction with Chase, the bank remarked, and “this is not a systemic issue.” Its customers still have access to their accounts and funds.

Reports about Chase on Downdetector peaked at nearly 5,000 around 2pm ET, Forbes reported. Customers of Bank of America, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank and Zelle also logged problems, in smaller numbers.

Recent findings suggest that of the Chase reports, 52% concerned balances and transactions, 21% web log-ins and 17% transfers, The Hill found. Chase’s app told customers that some services were not available.

Some Chase customers noted on social media they could not use ATM cards or finish transactions in branches. The New York Times reported those posts.

Industry observers note that the problems centred on Chase customers and on people at other banks trying to transact with Chase, the Associated Press reported. Bank of America and Citigroup had no problems with their own services, according to the AP.

In a recent development, “U.S. Bank systems are not directly impacted. However, clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays,” U.S. Bank stated in a statement.

Zelle, which a group of banks owns through Early Warning Services, said its network was working.

“An issue at the financial institutions may be impacting broader services, including Zelle payments, for some of their customers,” a Zelle spokesperson said.

The Federal Reserve’s own payment systems, including ACH transfers between banks, kept working normally. ABC News reported that from the Fed’s website.

Recent findings suggest that the banking problems followed Microsoft’s Outlook outage, which began on Thursday. Microsoft called that a technical problem, unrelated to cyberattacks or AI agents.

It has been revealed that on Friday, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop after its air traffic communications network failed. Officials traced it to an Arkansas router upgrade. The FAA said it was not a cybersecurity incident either.

There was no indication the bank problems were related to Microsoft’s outage, The Newly introduced York Times reported.
