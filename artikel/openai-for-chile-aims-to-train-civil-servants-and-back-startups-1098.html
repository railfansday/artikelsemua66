OpenAI for Chile aims to train civil servants and back startups

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that openAI has signed a memorandum of understanding with Chile’s science ministry to explore AI in public administration, train civil servants and support local startups. The deal sets no investment or purchasing commitment, and is backed by a US Commerce Department programme that promotes American AI abroad.

OpenAI has signed an agreement with Chile’s government to bring AI into public offices and help local startups, the tech giant announced on Tuesday. Science minister Ximena Lincolao signed for Chile at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World conference in California, where Sam Altman also spoke.

Industry observers note that the two sides will explore using AI in the science ministry’s paperwork and training civil servants to use it safely. Startups could get help from OpenAI, including tech advice and mentoring. A joint team will set goals and a first work plan.

The deal is a non-binding pact, not a contract. The details, including what each side provides, will be agreed later. Abu Dhabi, by contrast, signed four deals with OpenAI this month, covering permits and investments.

“The MoU sets no investment or purchasing commitment,” OpenAI wrote.

It has been revealed that the pact is backed by the US Commerce Department’s American SI Exports Program, which helps US AI firms win deals abroad. OpenAI is one of its anchor partners. Washington has also pushed federal financing for American AI exports.

Chile is the latest country to join OpenAI for Countries. Malta agreed in May to give citizens free ChatGPT Plus, and OpenAI’s head of countries, George Osborne, has courted Canada for data centres. Chile already has its own AI plan, with 177 actions across 14 ministries.
